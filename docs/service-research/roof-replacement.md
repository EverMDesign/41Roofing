## 1. What the Service Is

### Definition
Roof replacement is the complete removal of an existing roofing system down to the roof deck and the installation of a new system in its place. It is distinct from a roof repair, which addresses isolated damaged sections, and from a roof overlay, which installs new shingles over existing ones.

### Full Tear-Off vs. Overlay
**Full tear-off** is the industry-standard approach and the only method recommended for most residential roofing projects:
- All existing shingles, underlayment, and flashing are stripped to the bare decking.
- The deck is inspected and damaged sheathing is replaced.
- A complete new system is installed: underlayment, flashing, starter strips, drip edge, shingles, ridge cap, and ventilation components.
- Adds weight equivalent to only one layer of roofing material, keeping the structure within load tolerances.
- Required by most building codes (Texas included) when two or more existing layers are already present.
- Allows full inspection of the deck for hidden rot, mold, or structural damage — critical after storm events.

**Overlay (re-roof)** installs a second layer of shingles directly on top of the existing layer:
- Lower upfront cost; no tear-off labor or disposal fees.
- Hides the condition of the deck and existing underlayment.
- Traps heat between layers — a significant liability in Texas climates where attic temperatures already exceed 150°F.
- Voids most manufacturer shingle warranties.
- Not eligible for insurance replacement claims.
- Most DFW-area contractors and building departments discourage or prohibit this approach on existing two-layer roofs.
- Generally not recommended for Texas homes given climate conditions and code considerations.

### When Replacement Is Needed vs. Repair
Replacement is typically warranted when:
- The roof has reached or exceeded its expected service life (see Section 3 for Texas-specific lifespans).
- Storm damage (hail, wind) affects more than 25–30% of the total roof area.
- An insurance adjuster has documented damage sufficient for a full replacement claim.
- Multiple layers of shingles already exist (two layers is typically the maximum allowed).
- The deck has widespread rot, soft spots, or structural compromise.
- Granule loss is systemic across the entire field of shingles, leaving asphalt exposed to UV.
- Shingles are curling, cupping, or cracking across most surfaces — signs of end-of-life degradation.
- Repeated leaks occur in multiple locations despite prior repairs.
- Moss, algae, or mold has penetrated the shingle mat and underlayment.

Repair is appropriate when:
- Damage is isolated to a small section (a few shingles, one penetration flashing, one valley).
- The roof is relatively new (under half its expected lifespan).
- No widespread granule loss, curling, or systemic degradation is present.

## 2. What They Do — The Roof Replacement Process

### Step 1: Initial Inspection
A thorough inspection precedes any replacement estimate. For 41 Roofing & Restoration, this includes:
- **Exterior inspection**: Walking the roof to assess shingle condition, granule loss, curling, cracking, impact damage (hail strikes appear as soft depressions with granule displacement), ridge condition, flashing integrity, drip edges, gutters, and fascia.
- **Attic inspection**: Checking deck boards for water stains, rot, mold, sagging, and nail pops from thermal expansion. Evaluating ventilation (intake vs. exhaust balance).
- **Documentation**: Photo documentation of all damage. For insurance claims, this documentation becomes part of the claim file submitted to the adjuster.
- **Measurement**: Roof area calculated in squares (1 square = 100 sq ft) including pitch factor, for accurate material ordering.

### Step 2: Insurance Coordination (When Applicable)
In DFW, a large percentage of replacements are insurance-driven storm claims:
- The contractor works with the homeowner to file the claim or supplement an existing one.
- A public adjuster or the roofing contractor can accompany the insurance adjuster during the inspection.
- Scope of work is negotiated based on the adjuster's estimate (Xactimate is the standard estimating platform used by most carriers).
- Supplement items — missed line items, code upgrades, updated material costs — are negotiated before work begins.

### Step 3: Material Selection
Homeowner selects roofing materials based on budget, HOA requirements, insurance settlement, and performance needs:
- Shingle style (3-tab, architectural/dimensional, designer/luxury, impact-resistant)
- Color (must match or coordinate with exterior; HOA may restrict options)
- Brand and product line
- Underlayment type (synthetic vs. felt)
- Optional upgrades: ridge vent, ventilation baffles, ice-and-water shield at eaves and valleys

### Step 4: Permits
Most Texas municipalities require a building permit for full roof replacement. In Crowley and surrounding Tarrant County jurisdictions:
- Permit is pulled by the contractor (not the homeowner).
- Inspection by the local building department occurs after completion.
- Working without a permit can void homeowner's insurance coverage and create problems at resale.

### Step 5: Tear-Off
- Shingles, underlayment, and flashing are stripped from the deck using roofing shovels and tear-off forks.
- Debris is loaded directly into a dump trailer or dumpster positioned at the perimeter.
- Landscaping, AC units, and other vulnerable areas are protected with tarps.
- Nails remaining in the deck are removed or driven flush.
- Typical tear-off for an average DFW single-story home (25–30 squares) takes 4–6 hours for a full crew.

### Step 6: Deck Inspection and Repair
With the deck exposed:
- Each sheathing panel (typically 7/16" or 1/2" OSB, or older plank decking) is inspected by hand and visually.
- Soft, rotted, delaminated, or structurally compromised panels are replaced.
- In Texas, it is common to find heat-degraded OSB near ridge lines and at south- and west-facing slopes where summer temperatures are most extreme.
- Any failed nailing patterns are corrected per current code.
- Deck must be dry, flat, and structurally sound before proceeding.

### Step 7: Drip Edge Installation
- Metal drip edge is installed along eaves (bottom edges) first, before underlayment.
- After underlayment is laid, drip edge is applied along rakes (side edges).
- Texas wind uplift codes typically require drip edge to be fastened at specific intervals; Tarrant County follows IRC and local amendments.
- Drip edge directs water away from fascia into gutters and prevents wind-driven water infiltration at roof edges.

### Step 8: Ice-and-Water Shield (Where Required)
- Self-adhering rubberized membrane applied at eaves, valleys, and around all penetrations.
- In North Texas, full ice-and-water coverage is not always required by code (the region's freeze cycles are less severe than northern climates), but many contractors install it at eaves as a best practice given occasional winter ice storms.
- Valleys typically receive full ice-and-water coverage given the concentrated water flow volume during DFW's heavy rain events.

### Step 9: Underlayment Installation
- Synthetic underlayment (polyethylene or polypropylene) is the current standard in Texas, replacing traditional 15# or 30# felt paper.
- Synthetic underlayment is significantly more resistant to heat, UV exposure during open installation periods, and wind blow-off.
- Installed in horizontal courses from eave to ridge, overlapped per manufacturer specs.
- Serves as the secondary water barrier if the primary shingle layer is breached.

### Step 10: Flashing Installation
- All penetrations and transitions receive new metal flashing: pipe boots, chimneys, skylights, roof-to-wall intersections, dormers.
- Valley flashing: Open metal valleys or woven shingle valleys depending on roof geometry and preference.
- Chimney counter-flashing and step flashing are critical points of failure on older DFW homes; new flashing is installed with each replacement.
- Lead or galvanized pipe boots replaced with new rubber or lead units per pipe diameter.

### Step 11: Starter Strip Installation
- Pre-manufactured starter strip (or starter-cut shingles) is installed at all eaves and rakes.
- Provides a solid adhesive edge to bond the first course of field shingles.
- Critical for wind resistance; the eave and rake edges are the highest-risk areas for wind uplift in Texas storm events.

### Step 12: Shingle Installation
- Field shingles are installed in overlapping courses from eave to ridge per manufacturer's installation instructions.
- Fastening pattern follows code requirements; in high-wind zones (common across DFW), a 6-nail pattern per shingle is often specified over the standard 4-nail pattern.
- Texas building codes and many manufacturer warranties require specific fastener types, lengths, and placement zones.
- Alignment is checked periodically to maintain straight courses.
- Hip and ridge caps are installed last, covering the transition lines.

### Step 13: Ventilation System
- Ridge vent is installed at the peak, replacing solid ridge cap shingles or existing ridge vent.
- Soffit vents (intake) must be confirmed clear and functional; blocked soffits negate ridge vent effectiveness.
- Proper attic ventilation ratio: 1 sq ft of net free ventilation area per 150 sq ft of attic floor area (IRC standard), or 1:300 with vapor barrier.
- In Texas, adequate ventilation is directly tied to shingle longevity — trapped attic heat accelerates shingle degradation.
- Power attic ventilators, gable vents, or box vents may be evaluated for supplemental exhaust if ridge vent alone is insufficient.

### Step 14: Cleanup
- Crew performs a full cleanup of the property: all torn-off materials, scrap shingles, packaging, and loose nails.
- Magnetic roller or nail magnet is swept through the yard, driveway, and around the home's perimeter to collect loose fasteners.
- Gutters are cleaned of shingle debris and granules.
- Property walkthrough with homeowner prior to crew departure.

### Step 15: Final Inspection
- Contractor performs a final quality inspection of the completed roof.
- Fastener patterns, flashing integrity, shingle alignment, ridge line, and cleanup are verified.
- Building department inspection occurs per permit requirements.
- Homeowner walkthrough with photo documentation of completed work.
- Warranty documentation provided (manufacturer product warranty + contractor workmanship warranty).

## 3. What They Specialize In — DFW and Crowley, TX Specifics

### Texas Weather Factors

**Hail**
DFW is consistently ranked among the most hail-damaged metro areas in the United States. The region sits within "Hail Alley," where warm, moist Gulf air collides with cold fronts dropping from the Rockies, producing severe thunderstorms capable of generating large hailstones. Hail events of 1" diameter and larger are common multiple times per year; storms producing 2" to 3"+ hailstones occur with regularity across Tarrant, Dallas, and surrounding counties. Crowley and the southwestern DFW suburbs see significant storm activity.

Hail damage to asphalt shingles includes:
- Impact craters with granule displacement (exposing black asphalt substrate)
- Bruising (soft spots in the shingle mat)
- Cracked or split shingles from large stone impacts
- Damaged pipe boots, ridge caps, and metal flashings
- Dented gutters, fascia, and HVAC equipment (used as supporting evidence in insurance claims)

A single hail event can render an entire roof uninsurable for re-issue and necessitate full replacement. Many DFW homeowners cycle through full replacements every 10–15 years primarily due to storm events rather than age.

**Extreme Heat**
DFW summers regularly produce 100°F+ ambient temperatures, with roof surface temperatures on south and west-facing slopes reaching 160°F–180°F. This thermal loading:
- Accelerates asphalt oxidation, causing shingles to become brittle and crack
- Causes thermal shock — repeated expansion and contraction cycles weaken the shingle mat and adhesive strips
- Degrades standard 15# felt underlayment rapidly, reducing its service life to near zero in high-heat zones
- Shortens standard architectural shingle life from the nationally cited 25–30 years to 15–20 years in many DFW installations
- Bakes granules loose from the shingle surface, reducing UV protection prematurely
- Causes attic temperatures to exceed 150°F–160°F, stressing roof deck fasteners and OSB panel adhesive

**High Winds**
Straight-line wind events, derecho storms, and thunderstorm outflow boundaries regularly produce 60–90 mph wind gusts across DFW. Wind damage includes:
- Shingle tab lift and blow-off (especially on 3-tab shingles)
- Damage to starter strips and edge courses
- Failure of improperly fastened flashings
- Tree limb and debris impact

Texas winds also drive rain horizontally, testing flashing and underlayment details that might not be stressed in lower-wind regions.

**Heavy Downpours**
North Texas can receive several inches of rain in a matter of hours during severe storm events. Concentrated water flow stresses:
- Valley flashing and sealing
- Gutter capacity and attachment
- Drip edge installation quality
- Low-slope and flat roof drainage

**Tornado-Prone Region**
Tarrant and Dallas counties fall within North Texas tornado corridor. While direct tornado strikes are rare on any individual structure, near-miss events produce extreme wind loading, lofted debris, and uplift forces that can partially or fully remove roofing systems.

### Insurance Replacement Claims

Storm damage insurance claims are a primary driver of roof replacement in DFW. Key facts:

- Texas leads the nation in homeowner insurance claims volume, largely driven by hail and wind.
- A standard homeowners policy (HO-3 or HO-5) covers sudden and accidental roof damage from wind and hail. Most DFW policies include this coverage, though wind/hail deductibles (separate from the standard deductible) are common — often 1–2% of dwelling coverage or a flat dollar amount.
- **ACV vs. RCV policies**: Actual Cash Value policies pay replacement cost minus depreciation. Replacement Cost Value policies pay the full cost to replace. Homeowners with older roofs on ACV policies may receive significantly reduced settlements.
- The claims process typically involves: filing the claim, insurance adjuster inspection, estimate (Xactimate), contractor review and supplementation, contractor assignment, work completion, and release of recoverable depreciation (on RCV policies).
- Supplementing claims — identifying line items the adjuster missed or underpriced — is a standard part of DFW roofing contractor practice. Common missed items: code upgrade line items, steep slope charges, waste factors, updated material pricing, and ventilation upgrades.
- Texas Department of Insurance regulates contractor conduct and prohibits contractors from waiving or absorbing the homeowner's deductible; homeowners are contractually obligated to pay their deductible.
- After two or more claims in a policy period, carriers may non-renew coverage or increase premiums substantially. This is common in DFW markets.

### Material Considerations for Texas Climate

**Impact-Resistant Shingles (Class 4 Hail Rating)**
Class 4 is the highest impact resistance rating under UL 2218 testing standards. A steel ball is dropped onto the shingle from specified heights to simulate hailstone impact; Class 4 shingles withstand a 2" steel ball dropped from 20 feet with no cracking.

In Texas, Class 4 shingles are highly relevant because:
- Many Texas insurance carriers offer premium discounts of 15–30% for Class 4 roofs (discount varies by carrier and is not guaranteed — homeowners should verify with their specific insurer).
- Increased durability against DFW hail events can extend the replacement cycle and reduce future claim frequency.
- Common Class 4 products: Owens Corning Duration Storm, GAF Timberline ArmorShield II, CertainTeed Landmark IR, Atlas Pinnacle Pristine.

**Cool Roof / Reflective Shingles**
Light-colored or specially coated shingles reflect more solar energy (higher Solar Reflectance Index), reducing roof surface temperature and attic heat gain. Benefits in Texas:
- Can reduce attic temperatures by 10–20°F.
- May lower cooling energy costs during DFW summers.
- Some products qualify for ENERGY STAR certification.
- Lighter shingle colors are naturally more reflective; some manufacturers offer granule coatings that improve reflectivity without limiting color choices.

**Synthetic Underlayment**
Standard in DFW professional roofing practice. Polypropylene or polyethylene synthetic underlayments resist heat degradation, are far more UV-resistant during open installation, and have better slip resistance for workers on steep pitches. Felt paper (15# or 30#) deteriorates rapidly in Texas heat and is considered an inferior product for this climate.

**Metal Roofing**
Steel (Galvalume or painted Kynar-coated) and aluminum metal roofing systems are increasingly popular in DFW for:
- Superior wind resistance (standing seam can be rated for 140+ mph)
- Longevity well beyond asphalt shingles
- Reflectivity (helps with cooling loads)
- No granule loss or shingle blow-off during hail events (though hail can dent softer metals and may still be insurable damage)
- Reduced maintenance long-term

Metal is significantly more expensive than asphalt upfront but may offer lower total lifecycle cost.

**Tile Roofing (Concrete and Clay)**
Tile is common on higher-end DFW homes, particularly in Tarrant County communities. Tile performs well in heat but:
- Is heavy (requires structural assessment before installation)
- Can crack from large hailstone impact
- Installation and repair require specialty expertise
- Clay tile is more durable than concrete tile in freeze-thaw cycles

### Texas Building Codes and Wind Uplift Requirements

Texas adopts the International Residential Code (IRC) with state and local amendments. Key points for Tarrant County and Crowley:

- **Wind design speed**: DFW is generally in a 115 mph (3-second gust) wind design zone under IRC/ASCE 7 mapping. Some areas may have higher designations depending on local amendments.
- **Fastening requirements**: IRC requires minimum 4 nails per shingle; many DFW jurisdictions and manufacturers require 6 nails per shingle for high-wind zones.
- **Two-layer limit**: IRC Section R905.2.7 limits asphalt shingles to two layers; most jurisdictions require full tear-off on a two-layer roof.
- **Drip edge**: Required at eaves and rakes per IRC R905.2.8.5.
- **Ventilation**: IRC R806 governs attic ventilation ratios; adequate ventilation is a code requirement, not optional.
- **Permits**: Required by Crowley and nearly all Tarrant County jurisdictions for full replacement; some municipalities also require permit for partial replacements above a certain threshold.
- **Wind uplift testing**: Shingles must meet ASTM D3161 Class F (110 mph) or ASTM D7158 Class H (150 mph) wind resistance ratings for Texas installations. Most dimensional and impact-resistant shingles meet these ratings when installed per manufacturer instructions.
- **Contractor licensing**: Texas does not have a state-level roofing contractor license, but many municipalities require registration or local licensing. Tarrant County requires general contractor registration for permitted work.

## 4. Subservices List

### Complete Replacement Systems
- **Full Tear-Off and Replacement** — Complete removal of all existing roofing layers to the deck, followed by full system installation. Standard scope for most residential replacements.
- **Storm Damage Replacement (Insurance Claim)** — Full replacement triggered by hail, wind, or other covered storm event. Includes insurance claim assistance, adjuster coordination, and supplementing.
- **Aging / End-of-Life Replacement** — Planned replacement of a roof that has exceeded its useful service life. No insurance claim; homeowner-funded.
- **Emergency Tarping and Temporary Protection** — Following a storm event, tarping exposed areas to prevent water intrusion until full replacement can be scheduled.

### Material System Upgrades
- **Architectural Shingle Upgrade** — Replacing 3-tab shingles with dimensional/architectural shingles for improved aesthetics, wind resistance, and longevity.
- **Impact-Resistant Shingle Installation (Class 4 UL 2218)** — Installation of Class 4 rated shingles; may qualify homeowner for insurance premium discounts.
- **Designer / Luxury Shingle Installation** — High-profile shingles mimicking slate or wood shake; longer warranties and premium aesthetics.
- **Metal Roof Installation** — Steel or aluminum standing seam or exposed fastener metal roof systems; full tear-off and new installation.
- **Tile Roof Replacement (Concrete or Clay)** — Full removal and reinstallation of concrete or clay tile roofing systems; requires structural verification.
- **Flat / Low-Slope Roof Replacement** — TPO, PVC, modified bitumen, or EPDM membrane replacement for flat or low-pitch sections; common on additions, porches, and commercial-adjacent residential structures.

### Structural and Deck Work
- **Decking / Sheathing Replacement** — Replacement of rotted, delaminated, or structurally compromised OSB or plywood panels identified during tear-off.
- **Roof Deck Board Replacement** — Applicable on older homes with plank (1x6 or 1x8 board) decking; replacing failed individual boards or full deck re-sheeting.
- **Structural Rafter or Truss Repair** — Addressing damaged framing members exposed during tear-off; often required after storm impact or long-term water damage.

### Underlayment and Moisture Protection
- **Synthetic Underlayment Installation** — Upgrade from standard felt paper to polypropylene or polyethylene synthetic underlayment for improved heat and UV resistance.
- **Ice-and-Water Shield Application** — Self-adhering rubberized membrane at eaves, valleys, and penetrations for enhanced leak protection.
- **Full-Deck Ice-and-Water Coverage** — Applied to the entire deck surface for maximum protection; used on low-slope areas or as a premium upgrade.

### Edge and Flashing Details
- **Drip Edge Installation / Replacement** — Metal drip edge at all eaves and rakes; prevents fascia water damage and wind infiltration.
- **Starter Strip Installation** — Pre-manufactured starter course at eaves and rakes; improves wind resistance at vulnerable roof edges.
- **Roof-to-Wall Flashing** — Step flashing and counter-flashing at all roof-to-vertical-wall intersections; critical on dormers, additions, and attached structures.
- **Chimney Flashing Replacement** — Step flashing, counter-flashing, and cricket installation (where required) at masonry and metal chimney penetrations.
- **Valley Flashing** — Open metal valley or closed woven valley installation; channels high water volume safely to gutters.
- **Pipe Boot Replacement** — New rubber or lead pipe collars at all plumbing vent penetrations.
- **HVAC Curb Flashing** — Re-flashing of rooftop HVAC curbs, exhaust fans, and mechanical penetrations during re-roof.

### Ventilation System Work
- **Ridge Vent Installation / Upgrade** — Continuous ridge vent replacing older box vents or solid ridge caps; improves hot air exhaust along the full ridge line.
- **Soffit Vent Inspection and Clearing** — Confirming intake ventilation is unobstructed to balance ridge vent exhaust; critical for ventilation effectiveness.
- **Ventilation System Upgrade** — Full evaluation and reconfiguration of attic ventilation to meet IRC ratios; may include adding intake vents, baffles, or additional exhaust.
- **Attic Ventilation Baffle Installation** — Channel baffles installed at eave bays to maintain airflow path from soffit vents to attic space; prevents insulation from blocking intake.
- **Power Attic Ventilator Installation** — Thermostatically controlled attic fan for supplemental exhaust in attics where passive ventilation is insufficient.

### Additional Components
- **Skylight Replacement During Re-Roof** — Existing or new skylight installation integrated into the re-roof scope; prevents future leak points and ensures warranty compliance.
- **Skylight Flashing Kit Replacement** — Manufacturer-specific flashing kits for existing skylights reused during re-roof.
- **Gutter System Replacement or Repair** — Gutters often damaged by the same storm event requiring roof replacement; replacement or rehang frequently performed in conjunction with re-roof.
- **Fascia and Soffit Repair** — Rotted or storm-damaged fascia boards and soffit panels repaired or replaced during re-roof when deck work exposes these areas.
- **Solar Panel Removal and Reinstallation** — Coordination with solar contractor for panel removal prior to tear-off and reinstallation after completion; required to access deck beneath array.
- **Satellite Dish and Antenna Relocation** — Removal, temporary relocation, and reinstallation of rooftop-mounted equipment during replacement.

### Insurance and Documentation Services
- **Storm Damage Inspection and Documentation** — Thorough inspection with photo documentation for insurance claim submission.
- **Insurance Adjuster Accompaniment** — Contractor present during adjuster inspection to identify and document all legitimate damage items.
- **Claim Supplement Preparation** — Identifying and submitting missed or undervalued line items in the adjuster's initial Xactimate estimate.
