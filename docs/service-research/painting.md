## 1. What the Service Is

Residential painting encompasses the preparation, priming, and application of paint or stain to the interior and exterior surfaces of a home. It is both a standalone cosmetic service and a functional one — paint is a primary barrier between building materials and the elements.

For a restoration contractor like 41 Roofing & Restoration, painting is frequently the final step in a repair or restoration project rather than an isolated job. Common sequences:

- **Storm damage repair** — After hail or wind damages siding, trim, soffits, or fascia, the repaired or replaced materials need to be primed and painted to match or refresh the rest of the exterior.
- **Water damage restoration** — Interior walls and ceilings affected by roof leaks, plumbing failures, or flooding require stain-blocking primers and finish coats after drying and remediation are complete.
- **Siding replacement** — New fiber cement, wood, or engineered wood siding arrives pre-primed but requires two finish coats. A full repaint is often recommended to unify old and new sections.
- **Wood rot repair** — Replaced fascia boards, window trim, and porch components need to be primed and painted immediately after installation to prevent moisture infiltration.
- **Mold remediation** — Areas treated for mold growth need mold-resistant primer and paint as part of the complete remediation process.

Because 41 Roofing & Restoration handles the upstream repair work, offering painting as part of the same scope eliminates the need for homeowners to coordinate a separate contractor and ensures color matching between repaired and existing surfaces.

## 2. What They Do — The Painting Process

### Surface Preparation

Preparation is the most labor-intensive phase and the primary determinant of how long a paint job lasts. Skipping or rushing prep is the leading cause of premature paint failure.

**Exterior preparation:**
- Power washing the entire surface to remove dirt, chalking (oxidized paint residue), mildew, and loose paint. On Texas homes, this is particularly important due to dust accumulation and biological growth from heat and humidity.
- Scraping and hand-sanding any areas where paint is peeling, bubbling, or cracking.
- Sanding glossy surfaces to improve adhesion for new paint.
- Caulking gaps around windows, doors, trim joints, corner boards, and penetrations. Exterior caulk prevents water intrusion and must be paintable (100% silicone caulk cannot be painted; painters use siliconized acrylic or polyurethane formulations).
- Patching cracks or holes in stucco, wood, or fiber cement with appropriate filler compounds.
- Replacing or spot-priming areas of bare wood or repaired surfaces before applying finish coats.
- Masking off windows, fixtures, electrical outlets, and landscaping.

**Interior preparation:**
- Moving and covering furniture and flooring.
- Filling nail holes, dents, and small cracks with spackling compound; sanding smooth.
- Skim-coating or texturing patches to blend with surrounding wall texture.
- Caulking gaps between trim and wall surfaces.
- Sanding glossy walls or trim if switching paint sheens.
- Applying stain-blocking primer to water stains, smoke stains, or tannin bleed-through from wood.
- Taping edges where wall meets ceiling, trim, or adjacent surfaces.

### Priming

Primer serves distinct functions depending on the situation:
- **Adhesion** — Promotes bonding between the substrate and finish coat, especially on bare wood, repaired drywall, or glossy existing paint.
- **Stain blocking** — Shellac-based or oil-based primers (e.g., Zinsser BIN, Kilz Original) seal water stains, smoke damage, and tannin bleed-through that latex primers cannot block.
- **Mold resistance** — Specialty mold-resistant primers (e.g., Zinsser Mold Killing Primer) are used in bathrooms, laundry rooms, and areas that experienced water intrusion.
- **Color transition** — When making a dramatic color change, a tinted primer reduces the number of finish coats required.

Not every job requires a dedicated primer coat. Self-priming paints can work on previously painted surfaces in good condition, but a separate primer is typically recommended after repairs, on new construction, or after water damage.

### Paint Selection

Choosing the correct paint type for the surface and environment is a functional decision, not just an aesthetic one.

**Exterior paints:**
- 100% acrylic latex is the standard for exterior residential work. It expands and contracts with temperature changes without cracking, resists mildew, and cleans up with water.
- Flat/matte finishes are used on siding body areas to hide surface imperfections.
- Satin or semi-gloss finishes are used on trim, doors, windows, and soffits for durability and ease of cleaning.
- High-gloss is occasionally used on front doors or accent elements.

**Interior paints:**
- Flat/matte for ceilings and low-traffic areas where hiding imperfections is the priority.
- Eggshell or satin for living rooms and bedrooms — washable without being overly shiny.
- Semi-gloss for kitchens, bathrooms, and trim — the most moisture- and stain-resistant standard option.
- High-gloss for doors and cabinets where maximum durability and a furniture-like finish are desired.

**Specialty products:**
- Mold-resistant paints (e.g., Zinsser Perma-White, Sherwin-Williams Duration with Antimicrobial) for bathrooms and utility spaces.
- Elastomeric paint for stucco and masonry — a thick, flexible coating that bridges hairline cracks.
- Masonry paint for brick and block when painting rather than staining.
- Cabinet and furniture-specific paints (e.g., Benjamin Moore Advance, Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane) for hard, factory-like finishes on kitchen cabinets.

### Application Methods

**Brush:** Used for cutting in edges, painting trim and doors, detail work, and anywhere precision is needed. Leaves brush marks if used over large open areas; the appropriate tool for trim, not field application.

**Roller:** The standard method for painting walls and ceilings. Nap thickness varies by surface texture — smooth surfaces use 3/8" nap, heavily textured surfaces use 3/4" or thicker. Produces a consistent stippled texture.

**Airless sprayer:** Used for exteriors, cabinet painting, and large projects where speed and an ultra-smooth finish are priorities. Requires thorough masking of all adjacent surfaces. Typical process on exteriors is spray application followed by back-rolling to work the paint into the surface texture and ensure uniform coverage.

Most exterior painting uses a spray-and-back-roll technique. Interior walls are typically rolled. Trim is typically brushed or sprayed and back-brushed.

### Number of Coats

- **Standard finish coat:** Two coats are the professional standard in most situations.
- **Over dark or saturated colors:** May require three coats when going to a lighter color.
- **New or repaired surfaces:** One coat of primer plus two finish coats.
- **Ceilings:** Often one coat of ceiling paint is sufficient unless there is staining.
- **High-quality paints** (e.g., Sherwin-Williams Emerald, Benjamin Moore Aura) have higher hide and may achieve acceptable coverage in fewer coats than standard-grade products.

### Drying and Cure Times

Latex paints are typically dry to the touch in 1-2 hours under normal conditions, but require 4-6 hours between coats for sufficient film build. Full cure (hardening to maximum durability) takes 2-4 weeks for latex, longer for oil-based or alkyd products.

Applying a second coat too soon traps solvents and causes wrinkling, peeling, or soft spots. Temperature and humidity directly affect drying time — high humidity and low temperatures slow drying significantly.

### Cleanup

- Latex paints: brushes, rollers, and equipment cleaned with soap and water before paint dries.
- Oil-based paints and primers: require mineral spirits or paint thinner for cleanup.
- Masking tape removed while paint is still slightly wet to prevent pulling dried paint edges.
- Drop cloths, plastic sheeting, and overspray collected and disposed of properly.
- Touch-up paint stored and labeled for the homeowner for future spot repairs.

## 3. What They Specialize In — DFW and North Texas Specifics

### Texas Heat and UV Degradation

The DFW area routinely experiences 100°F+ temperatures from June through September, with direct sun exposure that is significantly more intense than in northern climates. This environment accelerates several paint failure modes:

**UV degradation (fading and chalking):** Ultraviolet radiation breaks down the binders and pigments in exterior paint. The visible result is fading (color loss) and chalking — a chalky, powdery residue that rubs off on contact. Chalking paint loses its protective function and becomes a poor adhesion surface for repainting. Paints with UV-resistant formulations (often marketed as "fade-resistant" or containing UV-blocking pigments) hold color measurably longer in Texas conditions.

**Thermal cycling:** Dallas-Fort Worth experiences wide temperature swings — 100°F summer highs and occasional sub-freezing winter temperatures. Paint films expand and contract with the substrate through this cycle. Cheaper or lower-quality paint formulations lose flexibility over time and crack or peel as the substrate moves beneath them. 100% acrylic latex maintains flexibility through a wider temperature range than vinyl-acrylic or alkyd formulations.

**Blistering:** When paint is applied to hot surfaces in direct sun, or when moisture is present beneath the paint film, blistering occurs. Bubbles form as trapped moisture or solvent vapors escape. This is a common consequence of painting during the hottest part of the day in summer without proper surface temperature management.

**Mildew and algae growth:** North Texas humidity, particularly from spring through fall, promotes biological growth on exterior surfaces — most visibly on north-facing walls and shaded areas that retain moisture. Quality exterior paints include mildewcide additives to resist this.

### Best Paint Types for the Texas Climate

- **100% acrylic latex:** The correct choice for virtually all DFW exterior applications. More flexible, more moisture-resistant, and more UV-stable than vinyl-acrylic blends.
- **UV-resistant formulations:** Sherwin-Williams Emerald Exterior, Benjamin Moore Aura Exterior, and similar premium products incorporate UV-blocking chemistry that extends color retention. The cost difference is justified by longer repaint cycles in Texas conditions.
- **Elastomeric coatings:** For stucco or masonry surfaces, elastomeric paint provides a thick, flexible membrane that accommodates the minor cracking common in stucco in Texas soil conditions. Applied by brush or roller in multiple thick coats; not suitable for wood.
- **Mold-resistant products:** In bathrooms, utility rooms, and anywhere water damage has occurred, mold-resistant primers and paints (e.g., Sherwin-Williams Duration Home with antimicrobial, Zinsser Perma-White) slow regrowth.
- **Direct-to-metal (DTM) paints:** For metal surfaces — garage doors, gutters, metal trim — DTM products bond without a separate primer and resist rust.

### Optimal Painting Seasons in DFW

**Spring (March–May) and Fall (October–November)** are the best painting windows in North Texas for both interior and exterior work.

- Temperatures in the 50°F–85°F range allow paint to flow, level, and cure properly.
- Humidity is moderate — paint dries at the correct rate without flashing too quickly or staying wet too long.
- Direct sun is less intense than summer, reducing the risk of blistering on hot surfaces.

**Summer (June–September)** presents challenges for exterior painting:
- Surface temperatures on south- and west-facing walls can exceed 140°F in afternoon sun — well above the recommended application temperature for most paints (typically 50°F–90°F surface temperature).
- Paint applied to overheated surfaces dries before it can properly wet the substrate, reducing adhesion.
- Solvents flash off too quickly, leaving brush and lap marks.
- Practical workaround: exterior painting in summer requires early morning application (before 10 a.m.) on shaded elevations only, moving around the house to avoid direct sun. Many contractors avoid large exterior projects from July through August entirely.

**Winter (December–February)** also has constraints:
- Paint should not be applied when temperatures are below 50°F (some products specify 40°F minimum) or when temperatures will drop below freezing within 24 hours of application.
- Cold slows curing, which can result in poor adhesion, soft films, and extended vulnerability to rain or condensation.
- DFW winters are mild enough that winter painting is feasible during stretches of moderate weather, but freezes can interrupt work unexpectedly.

### Humidity Considerations

Texas humidity, particularly in spring and during weather system transitions, directly affects paint performance:

- High relative humidity (above 85%) slows solvent evaporation and can cause latex paint to "blush" — a milky or hazy appearance.
- Paint applied when surfaces are still damp from dew, rain, or condensation will not adhere properly. Surfaces must be completely dry before application.
- Humid conditions extend drying times between coats; recoating too soon under high humidity causes film failure.
- Interior painting during very humid periods (windows open, no climate control) can result in the same adhesion issues; running HVAC to dehumidify and circulate air improves conditions.

### Storm Damage Repainting

DFW is in one of the most active hailstorm corridors in the United States. Hail and wind events create specific painting scenarios:

**Partial repair matching:** When only a section of siding, trim, or soffit is replaced, the new material must be painted to match the existing color. Exact matching depends on:
- The age and fade level of the existing paint (an exact color match from the original paint code may still look different against weathered surfaces).
- The number of years since the last paint job — heavily faded surfaces often require repainting the entire elevation rather than spot-matching.
- In some cases, insurance adjustments include repainting entire elevations or the full exterior to achieve a uniform appearance after partial repairs.

**Full exterior repaint after siding replacement:** When storm damage is extensive enough to require full siding replacement, a full exterior repaint follows. This is a complete-scope project: prep, prime, two finish coats on all exterior surfaces.

**Post-repair blending:** After wood rot repair or localized damage repair, touch-up painting is often insufficient because repaired areas absorb paint differently than aged surrounding surfaces. A full surface or elevation repaint produces a more uniform result.

### Common Exterior Surfaces in DFW Homes

**Hardie board (fiber cement siding):** The most common exterior cladding on homes built in DFW from the late 1990s through present. Arrives from the factory pre-primed; requires two coats of 100% acrylic latex. Factory finishes (HardieColorPlus) are baked-on and last significantly longer than field-applied paint, but field painting is standard practice for repaints and repairs. Fiber cement does not rot or warp, but the paint film is the primary moisture barrier — failed paint on Hardie allows water ingress and eventually causes substrate deterioration.

**Wood trim:** Common on older homes and as accent elements on newer construction. Requires thorough preparation (scraping, sanding, priming bare wood), high-quality primer, and a satin or semi-gloss finish coat. Wood is the most maintenance-intensive exterior surface because it moves with moisture content, which stresses paint adhesion.

**Brick:** DFW has a large stock of brick homes from the 1960s through 1990s. Options:
- **Leave unpainted** — The default; brick requires no paint maintenance and painting it is a permanent commitment since paint is very difficult to remove.
- **Paint brick** — Masonry paint or elastomeric coating applied over prepared, clean brick. Once painted, brick must be maintained and repainted periodically. Common on homes where brick has staining, inconsistent color, or owners want a fresh modern appearance.
- **Limewash** — A mineral-based coating (calcium hydroxide) that penetrates brick rather than forming a film on top. More breathable than paint; allows moisture vapor to pass through. Creates a textured, European or farmhouse aesthetic. Fades naturally over time and can be refreshed. Increasingly popular in DFW as a brick update option.
- **Staining** — Semi-transparent masonry stain penetrates and tints the brick while allowing texture to show through. Less opaque than paint.

**Stucco:** Less common in DFW than in West Texas or Arizona but present, particularly on custom homes and certain subdivisions. Stucco is porous, expands and contracts with temperature, and is prone to hairline cracking. Elastomeric paint is the recommended product — its thickness and flexibility bridges minor cracks. Standard acrylic latex can also be used but requires more frequent maintenance on stucco.

**Vinyl siding:** Vinyl cannot be painted with standard exterior paint without specialty preparation. Standard latex paint does not bond to the slick, flexible vinyl surface and will peel. If painting is necessary (typically to change color), a bonding primer formulated for vinyl followed by a paint specifically formulated for vinyl (or a light-colored 100% acrylic) is required. Dark colors on vinyl trap heat and can cause the panels to warp; manufacturers specify maximum LRV (light reflectance value) for painted vinyl. Generally, vinyl replacement rather than painting is the more durable long-term solution.

**Soffit and fascia (wood or fiber cement):** Frequently repainted as part of roofing and restoration work since these are the surfaces most affected by roof line damage, ice dam backup, and ventilation issues. Semi-gloss or satin finishes are standard for easier cleaning and moisture resistance.

### Interior Painting After Water Damage

When a roof leak, plumbing failure, or flooding damages interior ceilings and walls, painting is the final step after drying and any structural repairs:

**Stain blocking:** Water stains (yellow/brown rings on ceilings and walls) bleed through standard latex paint regardless of how many coats are applied. The correct process is:
1. Verify the surface is fully dry (moisture meter readings below 15% for drywall).
2. Apply a stain-blocking primer — oil-based (Kilz Original, Zinsser BIN shellac) for severe staining; water-based stain blockers (Kilz 2, Zinsser Bulls Eye 1-2-3) for lighter staining.
3. Apply one to two finish coats of ceiling or wall paint.

Skipping the stain-blocking primer and applying multiple finish coats is a waste of material — the stain will continue to bleed through.

**Mold-resistant primers and paints:** In areas where moisture intrusion occurred, even after remediation, applying mold-resistant products provides an additional layer of protection. Standard practice in bathrooms, laundry rooms, and any space that experienced flooding.

**Texture matching:** Drywall repairs after water damage often require re-texturing to blend with surrounding surfaces. Common DFW interior textures:
- Orange peel — the most common; applied by hopper spray or aerosol.
- Skip trowel — a hand-applied pattern; requires skill to match.
- Knockdown — a trowel-applied texture knocked down while wet.
- Smooth — common in higher-end homes; requires skim-coating over patched areas.
Texture must be matched before painting or repaired areas will be visible under any sheen of paint.

### HOA Color Requirements

A significant portion of DFW residential neighborhoods, particularly in Crowley, Burleson, Fort Worth, Mansfield, and surrounding communities, fall under HOA covenants that restrict exterior paint colors. Considerations:

- Most HOAs require homeowners to submit an exterior paint color change for architectural review committee (ARC) approval before painting.
- Approved color palettes are often limited to earth tones, neutrals, or specific manufacturer palettes.
- Even repainting to the existing color may require notification in some HOAs.
- Color changes made without approval can require the homeowner to repaint at their own expense.
- 41 Roofing & Restoration can document existing colors and help clients navigate HOA approval processes, but responsibility for obtaining approval rests with the homeowner.

## 4. Subservices

### Exterior Painting

**Full exterior house painting**
Complete repaint of all exterior surfaces: siding, trim, soffits, fascia, window casings, door frames, and accent elements. Includes full prep (power wash, scrape, sand, caulk, prime), two finish coats, and cleanup. Typically the most cost-effective approach when the entire exterior is due for repainting rather than patching sections.

**Exterior trim painting**
Window casings, corner boards, door frames, band boards, and decorative millwork painted separately from siding field color. Trim typically receives a semi-gloss finish for durability and contrast. Frequently done as a standalone service to refresh the appearance of a home without a full repaint.

**Soffit and fascia painting**
The horizontal (soffit) and vertical (fascia) boards at the roofline. These are among the first exterior elements to show paint failure due to heat accumulation under the roofline and exposure to water from gutter overflow or ice dam backup. Often painted in conjunction with roofing or gutter work. Standard finish: satin or semi-gloss white or off-white.

**Front door and entry painting**
Front doors and entry elements (sidelights, transoms, entry columns, porch ceiling) repainted as an accent or full color change. Front doors often receive a gloss or semi-gloss finish for a formal appearance and easy cleaning. Includes prep (sanding, deglossing, priming if bare), finish coats, and hardware protection.

**Garage door painting**
Steel or wood garage doors repainted. Steel doors require direct-to-metal primer or a DTM paint system. Wood garage doors follow the same prep process as wood trim. Color often matches trim or is a complementary accent.

**Deck and fence staining and painting**
Horizontal wood deck surfaces require products specifically designed for foot traffic and UV exposure — deck stains (semi-transparent or solid) or deck paints. Solid stains and deck paints provide a more opaque finish and hide weathering. Semi-transparent stains show wood grain and require more frequent reapplication. Fences can be stained or painted depending on the desired appearance. Pressure washing and brightening of weathered wood is part of the prep process. In DFW's climate, horizontal deck surfaces degrade faster than vertical surfaces due to standing water and direct sun.

**Shutters and accent painting**
Decorative shutters, window boxes, porch railings, and other accent elements repainted to complement or contrast siding and trim. Often done during full exterior repaints but can be a standalone refresh.

**Brick painting and limewash**
Preparation includes power washing, treating efflorescence (white mineral deposits) with masonry cleaner, and filling significant cracks or gaps in mortar. Brick painting uses masonry paint or elastomeric coating for a solid opaque finish. Limewash application involves wetting the brick, applying the calcium hydroxide mixture, and manipulating coverage for the desired look. Limewash requires less prep than film-forming paints and is more reversible.

**Stucco painting**
Power washing, repair of significant cracks with elastomeric caulk or patching compound, prime if bare stucco is exposed, and application of elastomeric paint or 100% acrylic exterior paint. Two coats minimum; elastomeric coatings are applied in heavier mil thickness and may require a single thick coat or multiple thinner passes.

**Porch and patio painting**
Covered porch ceilings (traditionally painted haint blue or white), porch floors (concrete or wood), and patio structures. Concrete floors require concrete-specific paint or stain with anti-slip additives for safety. Porch ceilings take flat or satin ceiling paint; many DFW homeowners paint tongue-and-groove porch ceilings a light blue-grey.

**Eave painting**
The underside of roof overhangs. Often painted at the same time as soffits and fascia. Can be a separate scope on homes where eaves are exposed rafter tails (craftsman or certain custom styles) rather than enclosed soffits.

**Chimney painting**
Masonry or stucco chimney surfaces repainted or waterproofed. Separate from chimney relining or flashing repair, but often coordinated with roofing work. Masonry sealer or elastomeric paint is appropriate for chimney surfaces exposed to rain, snow, and freeze-thaw cycles.

**Fence and gate painting**
Wood, metal (iron or steel), and vinyl fences. Wood requires prep (washing, sanding or stripping if paint is failing), primer, and paint or stain. Metal ornamental iron requires rust-inhibiting primer and exterior enamel. Vinyl fencing is rarely painted; if color change is desired, specialty bonding primer is required.

### Interior Painting

**Full interior painting**
All walls, ceilings, and trim in the home or in specified rooms. Typically priced and scoped room by room or by square footage of paintable surface. Includes all prep (patching, sanding, caulking, masking), primer where needed, and two finish coats on walls and trim.

**Accent wall painting**
Single-wall color treatment in a room. Requires careful masking of adjacent walls and ceiling. Often the same prep and application process as a full room but limited to one surface. Popular for adding color contrast without committing to full room color changes.

**Ceiling painting**
Ceilings are typically painted flat white or off-white. Prep includes patching any cracks or popcorn texture damage, addressing stains with stain-blocking primer, and applying one to two coats of ceiling-specific paint (formulated with higher solids for better hide and a flat finish to reduce glare). Popcorn ceiling removal can be added as a pre-painting service where desired.

**Cabinet painting and refinishing**
Kitchen, bathroom, and laundry cabinets repainted to achieve a fresh appearance or color change without replacing cabinets. This is a precision, multi-step process:
- Remove all doors and hardware.
- Clean thoroughly with deglosser to remove grease, wax, and silicone contamination.
- Sand all surfaces to break the gloss.
- Apply bonding primer.
- Apply finish coats of cabinet-specific paint (alkyd, urethane-modified acrylic, or conversion varnish) with a fine roller or sprayer to minimize brush texture.
- Allow full cure before reinstalling doors and hardware.
Cabinet painting requires significantly more labor and skill than wall painting; results depend heavily on product selection and application technique. Not a substitute for cabinet replacement when substrate is damaged.

**Interior trim and baseboard painting**
Window casings, door casings, baseboards, chair rail, crown molding, and wainscoting. Typically semi-gloss or gloss. Caulking all gaps before painting is essential for a clean final appearance. Often painted in coordination with wall repaints.

**Door painting (interior)**
Interior doors repainted, typically in semi-gloss or gloss. Doors can be painted in place or removed and laid flat for spraying. Laid-flat spraying or brushing produces fewer sags and a smoother finish than painting doors vertically in place. Includes hardware masking or removal.

**Stain blocking (water damage and smoke damage)**
Targeted application of stain-blocking primer to specific areas — ceiling water rings after roof repairs, smoke staining from fires or candles, crayon and marker stains, grease spots, and tannin bleed-through from wood. Followed by finish coat(s) to blend with surrounding surfaces. Often a component of water damage restoration completion work for 41 Roofing & Restoration jobs.

**Texture matching and repair**
Damaged, cracked, or patched drywall re-textured to match surrounding surfaces before painting. Specific texture types (orange peel, skip trowel, knockdown, smooth) require different application tools and techniques. Critical to the final appearance of any interior water damage repair.

**Wallpaper removal and painting**
Wallpaper removal is labor-intensive and is a prerequisite for painting walls covered in paper. Process: score the surface, apply wallpaper remover solution (steamer or chemical), remove paper in sections, clean remaining adhesive, repair damaged drywall underneath, skim-coat if drywall paper face was damaged, prime with oil-based primer to prevent paper face swelling, then apply finish coats. Skipping the priming step after wallpaper removal causes the drywall paper face to swell and bubble under latex paint — a common DIY failure.

**Garage interior painting**
Garage walls and ceilings painted in standard latex; concrete floors coated with epoxy floor paint or polyurea coating for durability, cleanability, and resistance to chemical spills. Floor coating systems require thorough concrete prep: acid etching or mechanical grinding to open the concrete surface, repair of cracks, and application of primer followed by color coat and clear topcoat. Standard floor paint (water-based epoxy paint) is a lower-cost option but wears faster than 100% solid epoxy or polyurea systems.

**Mold-resistant painting**
Bathrooms, laundry rooms, utility spaces, and any area with chronic moisture — painted with mold-resistant primer and finish paint. Used as part of the post-remediation process after mold treatment. Does not replace proper moisture control (ventilation, vapor barriers, leak repair) but provides a meaningful additional barrier. Products: Zinsser Perma-White, Sherwin-Williams Duration Home Antimicrobial Interior, Benjamin Moore Aura Bath and Spa.

**Color consultation**
Assistance selecting exterior or interior paint colors, accounting for the home's architecture, existing fixed finishes (brick, stone, roofing), HOA requirements, and neighborhood context. Color choice affects both aesthetics and practical performance — very dark exterior colors absorb significantly more heat in Texas summers, accelerating paint degradation and increasing cooling loads on the structure. Pulling and testing physical paint samples on actual walls before committing to full-room or full-exterior color decisions reduces the risk of dissatisfaction.

**Popcorn ceiling removal**
Scraping of spray-applied acoustic texture (popcorn or cottage cheese ceilings) common in DFW homes built from the 1960s through 1990s. Note: popcorn texture applied before 1978 may contain asbestos — testing is required before disturbing. Post-1978 popcorn texture can be wetted and scraped, leaving a base that requires skim-coating and priming before painting. Often done in conjunction with full interior paint projects.

**Fireplace surround painting**
Brick, stone, or tile fireplace surrounds painted or limewashed to update appearance. High-heat paint required for surfaces directly exposed to heat (firebox interior, damper). Standard exterior masonry paint is appropriate for the surrounding surround if it does not reach temperatures above 200°F.

**New construction touch-up and final coat**
Homes completing construction or major renovation often receive a builder-grade single coat of paint during construction. A finish-quality two-coat application, proper caulking, and touch-up of missed areas and damage from construction trades is a common service before move-in or listing.
