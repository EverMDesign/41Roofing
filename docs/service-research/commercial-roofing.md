## 1. What the Service Is

### Definition
Commercial roofing covers the installation, repair, maintenance, and replacement of roofing systems on non-residential or income-producing buildings. It also includes multi-family residential structures (apartment complexes, condos) where the scale and roof type align more closely with commercial construction than single-family homes.

### How It Differs from Residential Roofing

| Factor | Residential | Commercial |
|---|---|---|
| Roof slope | Steep-slope (4:12 or greater) | Low-slope or flat (0:12 to 3:12) |
| Primary material | Asphalt shingles | Membrane systems, metal, BUR, SPF |
| Project scale | Single structure, 1,500–3,500 sq ft | 5,000–500,000+ sq ft |
| Drainage design | Gravity via gutters and slope | Internal drains, scuppers, tapered insulation |
| Code authority | IRC (International Residential Code) | IBC (International Building Code) |
| Insurance process | Homeowner's claim | Commercial property claim, often with adjuster and risk manager |
| Decision makers | Homeowner | Property manager, facilities director, board, or owner |
| Project timeline | 1–3 days | Days to weeks depending on scope |
| Access requirements | Ladders, basic staging | Lifts, cranes, OSHA commercial fall protection |
| Warranty structure | Manufacturer + contractor limited warranty | NDL (No Dollar Limit) warranties, 10–30 year manufacturer-backed |

## 2. What They Do

### Inspections and Assessments
A commercial roof inspection is a systematic evaluation of the entire roofing assembly, not just the surface membrane. A thorough inspection covers:

- Membrane condition (punctures, blisters, shrinkage, seam integrity)
- Drainage performance (drain bowls, scuppers, downspouts, slope adequacy)
- Flashings (perimeter edge, penetrations, curbs, parapet walls)
- Insulation condition (thermal imaging can detect wet insulation without cutting)
- Substrate integrity (decking rot, fastener pullout, structural deflection)
- Penetrations (HVAC curbs, plumbing vents, conduit, pipe boots)
- Parapet walls and copings
- Skylights, roof hatches, and equipment platforms
- Evidence of ponding water (staining, algae, membrane deformation)

Inspections are recommended twice per year (spring and fall) and after any significant hail or wind event.

### Maintenance Programs
Preventive maintenance programs reduce long-term costs by catching minor issues before they become structural failures. A commercial maintenance program typically includes:

- Bi-annual or annual inspections with written report
- Drain and scupper cleaning
- Seam and flashing re-sealing
- Minor membrane repairs (punctures, small tears)
- Coping cap re-fastening
- Documentation and photo records for warranty and insurance purposes

Documented maintenance is often required to keep manufacturer warranties active.

### Repairs
Commercial roof repairs range from minor membrane patches to full flashing replacement or drain reconfiguration. Common repair categories:

- Membrane patching (cuts, punctures, foot traffic damage)
- Seam re-bonding or re-welding (TPO/EPDM)
- Flashing replacement (perimeter, penetration, curb)
- Drain repair or replacement
- Scupper clearing or enlargement
- Ponding water correction (tapered insulation, additional drains)
- Parapet wall rebuild or re-coping
- Blister repair in BUR systems
- Coating failure repair

### Replacements
Full commercial roof replacement involves removing the existing system down to the deck (or installing over existing layers where code allows), installing new insulation, and applying a new membrane or roof system. The process includes:

- Pre-job assessment and core cuts to evaluate existing insulation moisture
- Moisture scan (infrared or nuclear) to identify wet sections
- Permit acquisition per local jurisdiction
- Deck repair or replacement where needed
- Insulation installation to meet current energy code (IECC)
- New membrane installation per manufacturer specs
- Flashing and accessory installation
- Final inspection and warranty registration

Texas code (and most jurisdictions) limits the number of roofing layers before a full tear-off is required.

### New Construction
New commercial construction roofing involves working from a set of architectural drawings with specified system types, R-values, and drainage design. This requires coordination with the general contractor, mechanical trades (HVAC curb placement), and inspection scheduling tied to the construction timeline.

## 3. Common Commercial Roof Systems

### TPO (Thermoplastic Polyolefin)
- Single-ply membrane, typically 45–80 mil thickness
- Heat-welded seams (strongest joint type in single-ply category)
- White or light gray surface reflects UV — strong thermal performance in Texas heat
- Attached mechanically, adhered, or ballasted
- Cost-effective, widely used on low-slope commercial roofs
- Life expectancy: 15–30 years depending on thickness and maintenance
- Susceptible to puncture from foot traffic if not protected

### EPDM (Ethylene Propylene Diene Monomer)
- Rubber membrane, typically 45–90 mil
- Black (standard) or white-coated; black absorbs heat significantly
- Seamed with adhesive tape or bonded seams
- Highly flexible, performs well in extreme temperature swings
- Durable against UV and ozone degradation
- Less energy-efficient than TPO in Texas without white coating
- Life expectancy: 20–30 years

### Modified Bitumen Roofing
- Asphalt-based membrane reinforced with polyester or fiberglass mat
- Two main types: SBS (rubber-modified, cold or heat applied) and APP (plastic-modified, torch applied)
- Torch-down APP is common in DFW — provides very durable, waterproof surface
- Often installed in two-ply systems (base sheet + cap sheet)
- Cap sheets available with granulated surface for UV protection
- Works well on low-slope roofs with multiple penetrations
- Life expectancy: 15–25 years

### Built-Up Roofing (BUR)
- Multiple layers of bitumen alternated with reinforcing felts, finished with gravel or cap sheet
- One of the oldest commercial systems — proven long-term performance
- High dead-load weight — requires structural verification
- Excellent redundancy (multiple plies = multiple waterproof barriers)
- Gravel ballast provides UV protection and some impact resistance
- Labor-intensive installation; less common for new construction but still used in re-roofing
- Life expectancy: 20–30+ years

### Metal Roof Systems (Standing Seam)
- Steel or aluminum panels with concealed fasteners, raised seams
- Excellent longevity and low maintenance
- Handles steep-slope and some low-slope applications
- Strong wind uplift resistance when properly engineered
- Galvalume or Kynar-coated finishes resist corrosion in Texas climate
- Higher upfront cost; lower lifetime cost
- Well suited for churches, warehouses, retail, and office buildings
- Life expectancy: 30–50+ years

### Spray Polyurethane Foam (SPF)
- Liquid spray that expands into a rigid foam layer, then coated with elastomeric or silicone top coat
- Self-flashing — conforms to any shape, penetration, or irregular surface
- Provides both insulation and waterproofing in a single application
- Adds significant R-value (approximately R-6.5 per inch)
- Seamless surface eliminates traditional seam failure points
- Requires periodic top coat renewal (every 10–15 years)
- Well-suited for re-roofing over existing systems
- Life expectancy: 20–30+ years with proper recoating

### Roof Coatings
Not a standalone roof system — coatings are applied over an existing, structurally sound roof to extend service life. Types:
- **Silicone**: Best ponding water resistance; UV-stable; slippery when wet
- **Acrylic**: Reflective, cost-effective; not suitable for ponding water areas
- **Polyurethane**: High durability, foot traffic resistance
- **Butyl**: Good adhesion to metal; moisture-resistant

Coatings can extend roof life 10–20 years, delay capital replacement, and qualify for Cool Roof programs.

## 3. What They Specialize In (DFW / Crowley, TX Specifics)

### Texas Heat and Flat/Low-Slope Roofs
North Texas summers routinely produce ambient temperatures above 100°F. On a dark commercial rooftop — especially uncoated EPDM or aged built-up roofing — surface temperatures can reach 160–180°F. This accelerates:

- **Membrane oxidation and brittleness** — Asphalt-based systems dry out and crack; rubber membranes shrink
- **Seam and adhesive failure** — Heat cycles stress bonded seams repeatedly
- **Thermal expansion and contraction** — Creates fatigue at flashings, penetrations, and perimeter edges
- **HVAC stress** — A high-heat rooftop drives up cooling loads, increasing mechanical equipment cycling and energy costs

White TPO and reflective coatings can reduce surface temps by 40–60°F, extending membrane life and reducing interior cooling demands. For Crowley and the broader DFW area, specifying a reflective membrane or applying a cool-roof coating is one of the highest-ROI decisions a commercial property owner can make.

### Ponding Water on Flat Roofs
Ponding water is defined by most roof system warranties as any water that remains on the roof surface 48 hours after the end of a rain event. In North Texas, flat commercial roofs with inadequate slope or clogged drains are especially vulnerable.

Consequences of chronic ponding:
- Accelerated membrane deterioration (EPDM and modified bitumen are most affected)
- Added structural load (water weighs approximately 5.2 lbs per square foot per inch of depth)
- Algae and vegetation growth that root into membrane
- Drain bowl and flashing corrosion
- Interior leaks during heavy rain events

Solutions include drain relocation, tapered insulation installation, scupper additions, and in some cases structural re-sloping.

### Hail Damage to Commercial Properties
DFW is in one of the most active hail corridors in the United States. Commercial properties face disproportionate exposure compared to residential because:

- **Surface area**: A 20,000 sq ft warehouse presents 20,000 sq ft of uninterrupted target area
- **Membrane vulnerability**: Single-ply membranes (TPO, EPDM) can sustain bruising and punctures from 1" or larger hail that may not be visible on initial inspection
- **Metal system denting**: Standing seam metal roofs may sustain functional damage (dents at seams) or cosmetic damage affecting insurance value
- **Gravel displacement**: BUR systems lose gravel ballast in hail events, exposing the cap sheet to direct UV
- **HVAC and equipment damage**: Condensers, ductwork, exhaust fans, and skylights on the roof are often hail-damaged simultaneously

Hail damage to commercial membranes often requires infrared scanning or core sampling to quantify the full extent of damage — visual inspection alone is insufficient for large-surface claims.

### Wind Uplift on Large Flat Roofs
During North Texas severe weather events (including derecho-type straight-line wind events and tornado adjacency), large flat commercial roofs are subject to significant wind uplift forces. Key factors:

- **Edge and corner zones** experience uplift pressure 2–3x greater than the field of the roof
- Perimeter flashings, metal edge strips, and coping caps are the first points of failure
- Mechanically attached single-ply systems must be fastened to meet local wind speed requirements (DFW is ASCE 7-22 Wind Zone; most jurisdictions require design for 115–130 mph wind exposure)
- Ballasted systems are generally unsuitable for most DFW applications due to required ballast weight in high-wind zones
- After a wind event, the first areas to inspect are perimeter edges, parapet copings, and any previously repaired areas with adhesive flashings

### Energy Efficiency in Texas Climate
Texas commercial building owners pay significant energy costs due to long cooling seasons. Roofing decisions directly impact HVAC loads:

- **Cool roof membranes** (white TPO, reflective coatings): ENERGY STAR-rated products with Solar Reflectance Index (SRI) of 78+ qualify for utility rebates in some areas
- **Insulation upgrades**: Current Texas IECC (energy code) requires minimum R-25 for most commercial roof assemblies in Climate Zone 3 (DFW). Many older buildings fall well below this
- **Spray foam re-roofing**: Adds R-value while waterproofing — a two-in-one solution for buildings with aged low-slope systems
- **Thermal bridging at fasteners**: Mechanically attached systems can lose effective R-value through fastener conductivity — fully adhered or foam systems avoid this

### Commercial Insurance Claims After Storm Events
Commercial property insurance claims for roofing are more complex than residential claims. Key differences:

- Adjusters typically carry more technical expertise and may dispute damage classification
- Large-loss claims often involve independent adjusters or third-party engineering firms hired by the insurer
- Actual Cash Value (ACV) vs. Replacement Cost Value (RCV) terms affect payout significantly — many commercial policies default to ACV with depreciation
- Code upgrade coverage (Ordinance or Law coverage) may pay for bringing older systems into compliance with current code during replacement
- Business interruption and contents protection are often tied to roof condition documentation
- Photo documentation, pre-storm inspection records, and hail size verification (weather reports, hail pads) all strengthen a claim
- A contractor experienced in commercial claims can assist with scope writing, supplement negotiation, and adjuster meetings

41 Roofing & Restoration's DFW location means familiarity with regional storm patterns, local adjusters, and the Tarrant County / Johnson County building department processes.

### Texas Commercial Building Codes
Commercial roofing in Texas is regulated under:

- **International Building Code (IBC)** as adopted by Texas with state amendments
- **International Energy Conservation Code (IECC)** for insulation and cool-roof requirements
- **NRCA (National Roofing Contractors Association)** guidelines are the industry reference standard
- Local jurisdiction amendments: Crowley, Fort Worth, and surrounding DFW cities adopt IBC but may have local amendments affecting permit requirements, inspection scheduling, and material specifications
- **Texas Department of Insurance (TDI)**: Regulates contractor licensing requirements; roofing contractors operating in Texas should hold appropriate registrations
- **OSHA 29 CFR 1926 Subpart R**: Fall protection and safety standards for commercial roofing work
- Permit requirements: Most commercial roofing projects in Tarrant/Johnson County jurisdictions require a building permit, inspections, and in some cases engineering documentation (especially for new construction or structural deck changes)
