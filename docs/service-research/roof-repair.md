## 1. What the Service Is

Roof repair is the targeted correction of specific damage, deterioration, or failure points on an existing roof system — without replacing the entire roof. It addresses localized problems rather than whole-roof replacement.

A repair is appropriate when:
- The roof structure and decking are fundamentally sound
- Damage is confined to a limited area (typically under 25–30% of total roof surface)
- The roof has remaining functional life (generally more than 5–7 years)
- A full replacement is not warranted by the scope of damage or age of the system

Repairs range in scale from a single cracked pipe boot to large sections of damaged decking after a severe hail event. The goal is to restore the roof's ability to shed water, resist wind uplift, and protect the structure below.

Homeowners typically need roof repair after:
- A storm (hail, high winds, tornadoes)
- A detected interior water leak
- A routine inspection that surfaces aging or damaged components
- Visible exterior damage (missing shingles, exposed decking, sagging areas)

## 2. What They Do — The Repair Process

### Initial Inspection

Every repair starts with a full roof inspection, not just a look at the obvious damage. A thorough inspection covers:

- **Exterior surface**: Shingles or other field materials for impact damage, missing tabs, cracking, granule loss, lifted edges, cupping, or blistering
- **Penetrations**: Pipe boots, vents, HVAC curbs, skylights, and chimneys — all common leak entry points
- **Flashings**: Drip edge, step flashing, valley flashing, counter flashing at walls and chimneys
- **Ridge and hip**: Ridge cap condition, ridge vent integrity
- **Gutters and eaves**: Gutter attachment, fascia condition, soffit ventilation
- **Decking (from attic)**: Moisture staining, soft spots, sagging, mold — visible from inside before any materials are removed
- **Attic ventilation**: Improper ventilation accelerates shingle failure and contributes to ice dams in cold snaps

In the DFW market, hail damage inspections also include measuring dent diameter and depth on soft metals (vents, caps, gutters, A/C fins) as supporting documentation for insurance claims.

### Damage Documentation

Before work begins, damage is documented with photos and, where applicable, measurements. For insurance-related repairs, this documentation follows Xactimate or similar estimating standards and supports the claim file.

### Material Sourcing

Replacement materials are matched to existing roof materials as closely as possible — same shingle manufacturer and line where available, matching granule color. On older roofs where the original product line has been discontinued, the closest available match is used. Some repairs require partial replacement of components (e.g., a full valley strip rather than patching mid-valley).

### Repair Execution

The scope of work depends entirely on the damage type. General sequence for most residential repairs:

1. Remove and discard damaged materials (shingles, flashing, pipe boots, etc.)
2. Inspect exposed decking for rot, delamination, or fastener pull-through
3. Replace damaged decking if found
4. Install new underlayment in the repair zone
5. Install new field materials (shingles, flashing, boots, caps, etc.)
6. Seal penetrations and flashings per manufacturer specifications
7. Fasten per local building code requirements (Tarrant County / City of Crowley)

### Post-Repair Inspection and Cleanup

After repairs are complete:
- The repair zone is inspected for proper sealing, fastening, and material overlap
- A water test may be performed on active leak repairs to confirm resolution
- The work area and surrounding ground are cleared of debris, nails, and scrap material
- Magnetic nail sweeps are used on driveways and grass areas

### Documentation and Warranty

Completed repairs are documented with before/after photos. Workmanship warranties are issued separately from manufacturer material warranties. For insurance-funded repairs, a Certificate of Completion may be provided for the claim file.

## 3. What Makes Roof Repair Critical in the Crowley/DFW Area

### The DFW Climate Problem

North Texas subjects roofs to some of the most demanding weather conditions in the continental United States. The combination of hail frequency, extreme heat, thermal cycling, wind, and sudden severe weather events accelerates damage and shortens roof lifespans compared to more temperate regions.

#### Hail

The Dallas-Fort Worth metro is part of "Hail Alley," the stretch of the Great Plains where hail events are more frequent and more severe than nearly anywhere else in the country. DFW consistently ranks among the top U.S. metros for hail damage claims. Tarrant County (which includes Crowley) sees multiple significant hail events most years.

Hail damage to asphalt shingles is not always visible from the ground. Impact knocks granules loose, creating bare spots in the asphalt mat. These bare spots expose the underlying asphalt to UV radiation, which causes it to harden, crack, and eventually allow water infiltration. Functional damage can exist months before a visible leak appears. This is why post-storm inspections matter even when no interior symptoms are present.

Hail also damages:
- Ridge caps (exposed position makes them highly vulnerable)
- Pipe boots and rubber flashings
- Gutters and downspouts (dents affect drainage efficiency and are used as evidence of impact severity)
- Soffit vents and fascia
- Skylights and their curb flashings
- A/C condenser fins (used as corroborating evidence in insurance claims)

#### High Winds

North Texas regularly produces wind events in the 50–80 mph range during severe thunderstorms, and tornado activity is above the national average. Wind damage to roofs typically presents as:

- Missing shingles (tabs lifted and torn off)
- Lifted or creased shingles that have lost their adhesive seal strip bond
- Damaged or missing ridge cap shingles (most exposed area on the roof)
- Step flashing separation at wall intersections
- Soffit panel blow-out (soffit panels pulled loose or destroyed)
- Tree impact damage (branch strikes, whole tree falls)

Even shingles that appear intact after a wind event may have broken seal strips. Once the seal strip bond is broken, those shingles are prone to future uplift and will eventually allow water intrusion or be removed entirely in the next storm.

#### Extreme Heat and UV Degradation

DFW summers regularly exceed 100°F air temperature, and south- and west-facing roof surfaces can reach 150–170°F in direct sun. This sustained heat:

- Accelerates granule loss from asphalt shingles
- Dries out and cracks rubber components (pipe boots, EPDM flashings, caulk seals)
- Softens asphalt, making shingles vulnerable to scuffing and foot traffic damage
- Degrades sealants around penetrations
- Warps and deforms low-quality flashing metals

UV radiation compounds heat damage. North Texas has high UV index levels for most of the year, and UV exposure is the primary driver of asphalt oxidation. As asphalt oxidizes, it loses flexibility, becomes brittle, and develops surface cracking (alligatoring). This process is faster on darker-colored roofs and on roofs with inadequate attic ventilation that raises deck temperatures further.

#### Thermal Expansion and Contraction

North Texas temperatures swing dramatically — not just seasonally but within single 24-hour periods. A January cold front can drop temperatures 30–40°F in a matter of hours. These rapid swings cause repeated thermal expansion and contraction in roofing materials.

This cycling:
- Works fasteners loose over time (nail pops, lifted shingle tabs)
- Cracks brittle sealants at penetrations and flashings
- Fatigues metal flashings at bends and seams
- Opens gaps in ridge cap adhesion
- Stresses valley flashing at the seam between two roof planes

Repeated thermal cycling is why flashings and sealants around penetrations tend to fail faster than the field shingles on the same roof.

#### Heavy Rain and Drainage Problems

DFW receives an average of 36–40 inches of rain annually, often in concentrated intense rainfall events. These events produce high volumes of water quickly, stressing drainage systems and revealing any weaknesses in:

- Valley flashing (high water-volume areas where two roof planes meet)
- Gutter attachment and capacity
- Drip edge installation (or absence)
- Low-slope transitions on porch roofs or A-frame additions

Ponding water on low-slope sections accelerates shingle deterioration and overwhelms improper or degraded flashing seals.

#### Tornado and Severe Weather Exposure

Crowley and the broader Tarrant County area sit within one of the most active tornado corridors in Texas. Even tornadoes that do not make direct contact with a structure generate extreme pressure differentials and debris-field damage in surrounding areas. Near-miss tornado events routinely produce:

- Widespread wind damage across entire neighborhoods
- Flying debris punctures through shingles and decking
- Tree and limb impact damage
- Fence, fence post, and structure collapse onto roofs

### Insurance Context

Because DFW is among the highest-frequency hail markets in the country, most major homeowner roof repairs following storm events involve an insurance claim. This has practical implications for the repair process:

- Damage must be documented to insurance standards (photos, measurements, Xactimate estimates)
- Some repairs require a supplemented claim if initial adjuster estimates are incomplete
- Insurance-funded repairs must use materials that meet or match the existing system
- A public adjuster may be involved for large or disputed claims
- Homeowners' deductibles affect whether a repair or a full replacement is the economically sensible path

Roofing contractors working in the DFW market need to be fluent in insurance claim processes, adjuster communication, and documentation standards — this is not optional in this geography.

## 4. Subservices — Specific Repair Types

### Storm Damage Repair
General category covering repairs to any part of the roof system caused by a storm event (hail, wind, tornado, ice). Often involves multiple repair types from the list below completed simultaneously.

### Hail Damage Repair
Replacement of shingles, ridge caps, pipe boots, and flashing components impacted by hail. Includes granule loss assessment, functional vs. cosmetic damage evaluation, and insurance documentation. In severe hail events, can overlap with decking repair if hail penetrated through the shingle layer.

### Wind Damage Repair
Replacement of missing, lifted, or creased shingles. Re-adhesion or replacement of shingles with broken seal strips. Repair of ridge cap sections blown free. Repair of step and drip edge flashing lifted by wind.

### Leak Repair
Identification and correction of the specific entry point allowing water intrusion. Leak repair requires tracing the water path — the visible interior stain and the actual roof entry point are often not directly above each other. Repair includes fixing the failed component and inspecting surrounding areas for related vulnerabilities.

### Shingle Replacement
Removal of damaged, missing, or failed shingles and installation of matching replacements. Ranges from a few individual tabs to multi-square sections. Includes sealing of surrounding shingle edges and proper nailing pattern per manufacturer and code requirements.

### Ridge Cap Repair
Replacement of damaged or missing ridge cap shingles along the hip or ridge line. Ridge caps take the most wind exposure on the roof and are the highest-frequency wind damage item. Also includes inspection and repair of ridge vent components if integrated into the ridge.

### Flashing Repair (General)
Inspection and repair or replacement of any metal flashing on the roof — drip edge, step flashing, apron flashing, counter flashing, valley flashing. Improper or deteriorated flashing is among the leading causes of roof leaks.

### Chimney Flashing Repair
Repair or replacement of the flashing system at chimney penetrations. Chimney flashings are among the most complex flashing assemblies on a residential roof and are a common source of leaks. A properly done chimney flashing repair includes both base/step flashing and counter flashing, with proper embedding in mortar joints or sealed to brick surfaces.

### Valley Repair
Repair or replacement of the flashing or woven shingle system in roof valleys — the V-channel where two sloping planes meet. Valleys carry high water volume and are vulnerable to wear, ice damming in winter, and wind damage. Open valley metal repairs involve replacing the metal liner; closed valley repairs may require re-weaving shingles.

### Pipe Boot / Plumbing Vent Flashing Replacement
Replacement of rubber or metal pipe boot flashings around plumbing vent stacks. Rubber boots are among the shortest-lived roofing components — UV and heat degrade the rubber collar, which cracks and allows water to run down the vent pipe into the attic. A typical residential roof has 2–6 pipe penetrations. DFW's heat cycle makes boot replacement a common repair item on roofs 7–10 years old.

### Roof Vent Repair or Replacement
Repair or replacement of turtle vents, box vents, turbine vents, or power attic ventilators. Vents can be damaged by hail impact, can develop cracked or missing baffles, or can fail at their flashing collar. Turbine vents in particular are prone to hail damage that stops rotation and reduces ventilation effectiveness.

### Skylight Leak Repair
Repair of leaks at or around skylights. Skylight leaks originate from flashing failure (most common), cracked or failed sealants, or failed skylight glazing seals. Repair may involve reflashing the skylight curb, replacing the flashing kit, or in some cases replacing a failed skylight unit entirely.

### Soffit and Fascia Repair
Repair or replacement of damaged soffit panels and fascia boards. Soffit and fascia are frequently damaged by wind, debris impact, water infiltration from failed drip edge or gutter overflow, and animal intrusion. Damaged soffit compromises attic ventilation and provides entry points for pests.

### Drip Edge Repair or Replacement
Replacement of missing, bent, or improperly installed drip edge flashing at eaves and rakes. Drip edge directs water away from the fascia into the gutter and is required by most building codes and roofing manufacturer warranties. Absent or failed drip edge leads to fascia rot and undermining of the shingle starter course.

### Gutter-Related Roof Edge Repair
Repair of damage at the roof-to-gutter interface — pulled gutters tearing drip edge, overflowing gutters causing fascia rot, or improper gutter installation that has allowed water to wick back under shingles. May involve re-securing or re-sloping gutters, replacing fascia sections, and reinstalling drip edge.

### Decking / Sheathing Repair
Replacement of rotted, delaminated, or structurally compromised roof sheathing (OSB or plywood). Decking damage is typically discovered during shingle removal — it is rarely visible from the surface. Common causes include long-term slow leaks, inadequate attic ventilation (moisture accumulation from the interior), or severe storm impact. Structural damage to rafters or trusses is beyond repair scope and requires a separate structural assessment.

### Attic Ventilation Correction
Not a surface repair, but directly tied to roof longevity. Inadequate attic ventilation raises deck temperatures, accelerating shingle degradation, and allows moisture to accumulate in the attic, leading to decking rot. Ventilation corrections include adding intake (soffit) vents, adding exhaust vents, or replacing failed ventilation components.

### Emergency Patching / Tarping
Immediate temporary protection applied after sudden damage — a fallen tree, tornado pass, or severe storm that has opened the roof to the weather. Emergency patches use roofing cement, self-adhering modified bitumen peel-and-stick, or heavy-duty polyethylene tarps secured to the deck (not fastened to shingles). Emergency patching is a holding measure, not a permanent repair.

### Animal / Critter Damage Repair
Repair of entry points created by animals — most commonly squirrels and raccoons that chew through wood fascia, soffit, or roof decking to access attic spaces. Also includes repair of damage caused by birds nesting under lifted shingles or in damaged vents. Repair involves sealing entry points with materials animals cannot re-penetrate (hardware cloth, metal coil stock) in addition to replacing damaged roofing and wood components. Animal damage repair should coordinate with pest removal before sealing.

### Flat / Low-Slope Roof Repair
Repair of built-up roofing (BUR), modified bitumen, or single-ply membrane systems on flat or near-flat roof sections. Common on commercial buildings, porch additions, and some residential designs. Repairs include blister repair, seam resealing, drain flashing repair, and patching of punctures or impact damage. Low-slope sections are the highest-risk area for ponding water and require proper positive drainage to function.

### Caulk and Sealant Replacement
Replacement of failed caulk or sealant at penetrations, flashings, and transitions. Caulk and roofing sealants have shorter service lives than structural roofing components — typically 5–10 years in DFW's UV and heat environment. Sealant failure at any penetration becomes an active leak point. This is a maintenance-level repair, not a full flashing replacement, and appropriate where the underlying flashing is still structurally sound.

### Ice Dam Damage Repair
Less frequent than other types in DFW but relevant during hard freeze events. When the DFW area experiences extended freezes (as in the February 2021 winter storm), ice dams can form at eaves on homes with poor attic insulation or ventilation. Ice damming forces water back up under shingles, causing leaks and interior water damage. Repair involves addressing the resulting leak and shingle/flashing damage, and may include ventilation or insulation improvements to prevent recurrence.

## Reference Notes for Web Copy Development

- DFW averages 7–9 significant hail events per year, with some years exceeding 15
- Tarrant County consistently appears in national top-10 lists for insurance hail claims
- Asphalt shingle manufacturers typically warrant against hail impact for the life of the shingle, but these warranties require documented proper installation and may have exclusions
- Texas does not license roofing contractors at the state level (as of research date), making contractor vetting important for homeowners
- Crowley is within Tarrant County jurisdiction for building permits; permits may be required for repairs exceeding certain scopes (verify with Crowley Building Inspection)
- Ice and Water Shield underlayment is particularly relevant in DFW for valley areas and around penetrations given the combination of freeze events and heavy rain
- Most residential roofs in DFW are asphalt shingles; metal roofing (standing seam, metal shingles) is a growing segment with different repair considerations
