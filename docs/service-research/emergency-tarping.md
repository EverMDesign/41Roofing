## 1. What the Service Is

Emergency tarping is the process of applying heavy-duty protective coverings — typically reinforced polyethylene tarps — over damaged sections of a roof to prevent water, debris, and weather from entering a home until permanent repairs can be made. Board-up services extend this concept to openings created by fallen trees, structural collapse, or wind-blown debris, using plywood or structural panels to seal breaches in the roof deck or exterior walls.

This is a **temporary protective measure**, not a repair. The goal is damage containment: stop the active exposure, preserve what hasn't yet been damaged, and buy time for a proper insurance assessment and permanent restoration.

### When Emergency Tarping Is Needed

- A hailstorm punches holes through shingles and the underlying decking
- High winds peel back sections of roofing material, exposing the deck or open framing
- A falling tree limb or entire tree impacts the roof, creating a structural breach
- A sudden, severe leak develops during or immediately after a storm event
- Wind-lifted flashing or a failed ridge cap allows water intrusion at scale
- Tornado or microburst damage displaces large sections of roofing
- A skylight, vent, or chimney surround fails under storm impact

If the roof membrane — shingles, underlayment, or decking — is breached and rain is either actively entering or could enter the next time it rains, emergency tarping is warranted. Texas weather does not provide grace periods: the next storm can arrive within days.

## 2. What the Process Involves

### Rapid Response and Initial Contact

When a homeowner calls for emergency tarping, the first priority is establishing how severe the breach is and whether the structure is safe to access. The crew dispatch typically happens within **2–4 hours** of initial contact for daytime calls; after-hours calls may extend to **4–6 hours** depending on crew availability and active storm conditions. During active severe weather events across the Metroplex, response queues can extend, but 41 Roofing's local presence in Crowley means shorter drive times to southwest Tarrant County, Burleson, Mansfield, Kennedale, and surrounding communities compared to contractors staging from Dallas or Plano.

### Damage Assessment on Arrival

Before any tarp goes on, the crew performs a ground-level and roof-level damage assessment:

- Identify all breach points, not just the most obvious one
- Check for structural compromise that would make the roof unsafe to walk
- Assess the size and scope of damage to determine tarp dimensions needed
- Note interior damage visible from the attic or accessible ceiling areas
- Identify secondary vulnerabilities (damaged flashing, lifted ridge cap, cracked skylights) that need temporary addressing

This assessment is documented with photographs and video before any work begins. This pre-work documentation is critical for insurance purposes — it establishes the original condition of the damage before any mitigation disturbs the scene.

### Tarp Installation

Professional-grade tarps used in roofing emergency work are commercial-weight (typically 6–12 mil polyethylene or heavier woven poly), not the hardware store variety. Installation involves:

1. **Sizing the tarp** to extend a minimum of 3–4 feet past every edge of the damaged area, including past the ridge line when necessary to prevent water from running under the tarp
2. **Securing the leading edge** at the ridge or a structurally sound point above the breach
3. **Fastening edges and field** using a combination of batten boards (narrow wood strips screwed through the tarp into the decking), cap nails, or weighted anchor systems — the method depends on roof pitch, surface condition, and expected wind loads
4. **Sealing overlaps** where multiple tarps are needed for large areas
5. **Checking for uplift vulnerability** — any tarp edge exposed to wind must be mechanically secured, not just weighted

In Texas, where post-storm winds can continue for days and secondary thunderstorm cells are common, an improperly secured tarp creates a second emergency. Professional installation accounts for wind uplift, not just rain exclusion.

### Board-Up Services

Where structural framing has been exposed or debris has created an opening too large or irregular for tarping alone, board-up services use plywood panels (typically 3/4" CDX) cut and fastened to cover the opening. This is common after:

- Tree impact that collapses a section of decking and framing
- Tornado damage that removes entire roof sections
- Wind events that tear away dormers, gable ends, or fascia-connected sections

Board-up may be combined with tarping: the boards seal the structural opening, the tarp overlays to provide weatherproofing.

### Documentation for Insurance

After installation, the crew provides:

- Timestamped before, during, and after photographs organized for adjuster review
- A written scope of the visible damage observed
- Material and labor records for the tarping work (insurance carriers require this for reimbursement of mitigation costs)
- Assessment notes identifying the scope of permanent repair likely needed

This documentation package is handed to the homeowner and, where authorized, transmitted directly to the insurance carrier or adjuster.

### Scheduling Permanent Repair

Emergency tarping is not the end of the process. Before the crew leaves, the permanent repair scheduling conversation should happen: a full inspection is typically set within 24–72 hours to produce a detailed repair estimate, which feeds the insurance claim. The tarp should be treated as a temporary solution with an expected useful life measured in weeks, not months — UV exposure degrades polyethylene, and mechanical fasteners work loose over time, especially through Texas heat cycles.

## 3. DFW and Crowley-Specific Context

### North Texas Severe Weather Frequency

The Dallas-Fort Worth Metroplex sits within one of the most active severe weather corridors in the United States. Tarrant County — where Crowley is located — experiences a documented pattern of:

- **Large hail**: Quarter-size to baseball-size hail events occur multiple times annually across the county. In recent years, Tarrant County has recorded hail events exceeding 4 inches in diameter. Hailstones larger than 1.75 inches (golf ball) cause direct puncture damage to shingles and can penetrate through to the decking.
- **Straight-line winds**: Derecho events and bow echoes produce straight-line wind gusts of 60–80+ mph across the Metroplex. Gusts in excess of 100 mph have been recorded during downburst events, producing damage comparable to EF1–EF2 tornadoes over wide areas.
- **Tornadoes**: The DFW area experiences tornado activity with above-average frequency relative to national norms. Crowley and southwest Tarrant County sit in a corridor that has seen tornado touchdowns in multiple recent seasons.
- **Microbursts**: Localized downbursts from collapsing thunderstorm columns can produce explosive wind events affecting a single neighborhood or even a single block, making the damage pattern irregular and difficult to predict.

In the last 12 months of recorded data, Crowley, TX has logged over 300 storm reports with peak hail reaching 4.5 inches and peak wind at 89 mph — figures consistent with the broader Tarrant County pattern.

### Storm Season Timing

North Texas operates on two distinct severe weather seasons:

**Primary Season: March through June**
This is when the collision of warm Gulf moisture with cooler air from the northwest produces the most frequent and most severe thunderstorm activity. April and May carry the highest statistical risk. Tornado watches and severe thunderstorm warnings are routine occurrences across this period.

**Secondary Season: September through November**
As summer heat begins to break and the jet stream shifts, a second active period emerges. Storms in this window tend to be less frequent than spring but can be intense. The secondary season is often underestimated by homeowners who assume storm risk ends after summer.

Outside these windows, isolated severe weather events — particularly in warm Decembers or during La Nina patterns — are not unusual for North Texas.

### Why Rapid Response Matters: Mold and Heat

Texas heat and humidity create a compressed timeline for secondary damage after a roof breach.

Under standard conditions, mold spore germination can begin within **24–48 hours** of moisture intrusion. In North Texas summer conditions — attic temperatures routinely exceeding 130–150°F, combined with ambient humidity and trapped moisture — that timeline can be effectively shorter because the thermal cycling accelerates the drying-and-wetting pattern that spore colonies thrive in.

Practically: a roof breached on a Tuesday evening after a storm, left open through Wednesday and Thursday, may already have initiated mold colonization in the insulation, decking, and ceiling framing by the time a contractor arrives Friday. Mold remediation costs are typically not fully covered under standard homeowners policies without dedicated mold coverage riders, and the remediation work can dwarf the cost of the original roof repair.

The critical window is **the first 24–48 hours**. Emergency tarping within that window prevents the secondary damage cascade.

### Insurance Duty to Mitigate

Texas homeowners insurance policies — like virtually all U.S. property policies — include a **duty to mitigate** provision. This clause requires the policyholder to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage after a covered loss occurs. Failure to mitigate can give the carrier grounds to deny coverage for damage that occurs after the initial storm event.

In practical terms: if a storm creates a roof breach on Monday night and the homeowner takes no action, and then rain on Wednesday causes ceiling collapse and flooring damage, the carrier may only cover the original storm damage and dispute the water-intrusion losses as preventable.

Emergency tarping satisfies the duty to mitigate. The cost of professional tarping is typically covered as a separate line item in the insurance claim and is reimbursable — homeowners should retain all receipts and documentation from the tarping contractor.

Key points on insurance interaction:

- Photograph all damage **before** tarping begins — do not disturb the damage scene without documentation
- Do not perform full repairs before the adjuster inspection; temporary mitigation is appropriate, but permanent repairs before inspection can complicate claim approval
- Report the claim to the carrier within **24–48 hours** of the damage event
- Texas law generally provides one year from the date of damage to file a claim, but early reporting is strongly advisable

### The DFW Storm Chaser Problem

After every significant hail or wind event in the DFW area, out-of-state contractors — commonly called "storm chasers" — arrive in affected neighborhoods soliciting emergency and repair work. These crews follow storm tracks across the country, moving from market to market. The pattern is well-documented: the BBB consistently ranks storm chaser contractors among the most complained-about contractor types in Texas following major weather events.

The specific problems with storm chaser contractors for emergency tarping work:

- **No local accountability**: Once paid, they move to the next storm market. If the tarp fails in the next rain event, or if the installation damages additional roofing material, there is typically no local office to contact and no recourse.
- **No established relationship with local adjusters**: Local contractors develop working relationships with insurance adjusters active in the DFW market. Storm chasers do not.
- **Substandard materials and methods**: High-volume storm chase operations prioritize speed over installation quality. Tarps that aren't properly battened or secured can become projectiles in high winds, creating new damage.
- **Contract manipulation**: Some storm chaser operations use emergency situations to pressure homeowners into signing contingency agreements or assignment of benefits documents before the scope of work or insurance coverage is understood.
- **Warranty void risk**: If a storm chaser crew damages additional roofing material during tarp installation, proving liability becomes nearly impossible after they leave the market.

A local Crowley-based contractor has a physical address, established business history, Texas registration, and a direct stake in reputation within the community they serve. For emergency work — where speed and accountability both matter — local presence is not a preference; it is a practical risk management factor.

### Common Emergency Scenarios in North Texas

**Hail Punching Through Decking**
Large hail — golf ball size and above — can penetrate through shingles and the underlayment and crack or fracture the OSB decking below. This leaves a direct water pathway that no amount of shingle coverage can seal. These breaches are often invisible from the ground and require roof-surface inspection to identify.

**Wind Lifting Shingles and Decking Sections**
Straight-line winds and tornado-adjacent events can create negative pressure (suction) on roof sections, lifting shingles, underlayment, and even sections of decking away from the framing. This is distinct from wind driving rain under shingles — it is a structural separation that creates an open breach.

**Fallen Tree Limbs and Full Tree Impact**
The DFW area's post-oak and cedar elm canopy makes tree impact a recurring emergency scenario. Limbs weakened by drought stress — a chronic condition in North Texas — are prone to failure even in moderate wind events. Full tree falls during tornado events or straight-line wind events can collapse framing, not just breach the deck.

**Failed or Displaced Flashing**
High winds routinely displace ridge cap flashing, valley flashing, and step flashing at roof-to-wall intersections. Displaced flashing can allow significant water intrusion at points that would otherwise be watertight.

**Skylight and Chimney Damage**
Hail impact can crack skylight glazing or dome covers. Wind events can displace chimney caps, leaving an open vertical penetration into the home. Both require immediate temporary covering.

## 4. Subservices List

### Core Emergency Services

**Emergency Roof Tarping**
Application of commercial-grade polyethylene tarps over storm-damaged roof sections. Includes proper edge battening and mechanical fastening to withstand post-storm wind events. Sized to extend beyond the damage perimeter in all directions.

**Board-Up Services**
Structural closure of large roof breaches using plywood panels fastened to exposed framing. Used when damage is too extensive or structurally irregular for tarping alone, or in combination with tarping for layered protection.

**Temporary Leak Containment**
For smaller, localized leaks where full tarping isn't yet feasible (active storm conditions, unsafe roof access), application of temporary sealants, leak barriers, or interior containment measures to limit water intrusion until a crew can safely access the roof.

**Fallen Tree and Debris Removal from Roof**
Safe removal of tree limbs, branches, and large debris from the roof surface prior to tarp installation. This is a prerequisite step — tarping over an unstable tree limb creates structural risk and prevents accurate damage assessment. Includes hauling debris from the property.

**Storm Damage Documentation**
Comprehensive photo and video documentation of all visible damage, organized chronologically and formatted for insurance adjuster submission. Captures wide, medium, and close-up views of each damage area. Provides a written damage narrative. Completed before any tarping or mitigation work disturbs the scene.

**24/7 Emergency Response**
Dispatch availability for after-hours, overnight, and weekend storm events. North Texas storm activity does not follow business hours — peak severe weather often strikes in the late afternoon and evening. Emergency contact line staffed around the clock during active storm seasons.

### Structural and Opening Protection

**Emergency Skylight Cover**
Temporary plywood or heavy-duty tarp covering over cracked, shattered, or displaced skylight units. Hail impact is the primary cause of skylight failure during North Texas storm events.

**Chimney Cap Emergency Cover**
Temporary capping of open chimney flues after cap displacement or chimney crown damage. An open flue during rain events allows direct water entry into the firebox and surrounding structure.

**Wind Damage Securing**
Mechanical re-fastening or temporary securing of lifted but still-attached roofing sections — displaced ridge caps, lifted flashing, partially detached shingle fields — to prevent further wind damage before permanent repair.

**Gable End and Soffit Protection**
Temporary covering or securing of damaged gable ends and open soffit sections where wind damage has created lateral entry points for water and debris.

### Water Management

**Temporary Gutter Diversion**
When gutter systems have been damaged or detached and are directing water against the foundation or through wall penetrations, temporary diversion of downspout flow away from the structure. Addresses a secondary damage vector that operates between rain events.

**Interior Water Extraction Coordination**
For cases where water has already entered the structure, coordination with water mitigation specialists for interior extraction, drying equipment placement, and moisture mapping. Emergency tarping stops the inflow; water extraction addresses what's already inside.

### Insurance and Administrative Support

**Insurance Claim Filing Assistance**
Support for homeowners navigating the first-notice-of-loss process with their carrier. Includes communicating what information the carrier will need, what documentation has been compiled, and what the homeowner's obligations are under the duty-to-mitigate provision.

**Adjuster Inspection Coordination**
Scheduling and on-site presence during the insurance adjuster's inspection. The contractor's presence during adjuster walk-throughs allows real-time clarification of damage scope and ensures that damage identified during emergency assessment is not overlooked.

**Supplemental Damage Documentation**
After the initial tarp is in place, follow-up documentation of any additional damage discovered during the tarping process that may not have been visible in the initial assessment. Provided as a supplement to the original damage documentation package.

**Hail Size and Storm Date Verification**
Collection of storm event data (date, hail size reported by NOAA/National Weather Service for the property's location) to support insurance claim filing and establish that reported damage aligns with a documented weather event.
