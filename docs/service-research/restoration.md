## 1. What the Service Is

### Restoration vs. Remodeling

These terms are often confused but describe fundamentally different scopes of work.

**Restoration** is the process of returning a property to its pre-loss condition — or better — following damage. The trigger is always an event: a storm, a burst pipe, a fire, a flood. The goal is to make the property whole again structurally and cosmetically. Restoration work is almost always tied to an insurance claim.

**Remodeling** is elective improvement work performed on a property that hasn't necessarily been damaged. Updating a kitchen layout, adding square footage, or changing the exterior aesthetic are remodeling decisions driven by preference, not necessity.

The distinction matters practically because:
- Restoration is typically insurance-funded, not out-of-pocket
- Restoration work must match or exceed pre-loss condition (per most Texas homeowner policies)
- Restoration projects require documentation of what existed before damage and what it costs to replace it
- A licensed restoration contractor must understand scope of loss, Xactimate estimating, and the insurance claim process — not just how to swing a hammer

**Interior and exterior restoration** means the contractor handles the full property — not just the roof. After a major hail storm, tornado, freeze event, or fire, the damage rarely stays in one area. A roofing contractor who also performs interior and exterior restoration can manage the entire project under a single contract, single insurance claim, and single point of accountability.

## 2. What They Do

### The Restoration Process

Restoration follows a defined sequence regardless of whether the damage is interior, exterior, or both.

**Phase 1 — Damage Assessment**
A thorough inspection of all affected areas: roof, exterior cladding, structural elements, windows, doors, interior ceilings, walls, floors, and mechanical systems. The goal is to document everything, not just what's obviously broken. Hidden damage (moisture intrusion behind walls, compromised roof decking under intact shingles, cracked window seals) is identified here or discovered during demolition.

**Phase 2 — Scope of Work Development**
A line-by-line scope is built in Xactimate — the industry-standard insurance estimating software. Every item that needs repair or replacement is documented with materials, labor, and regional pricing. This scope becomes the basis for the insurance claim.

**Phase 3 — Insurance Coordination**
The contractor works alongside the homeowner and their insurance adjuster to ensure the full scope of damage is captured. This includes attending the adjuster's inspection, providing photo documentation, and submitting supplements (additional line items) when the initial estimate misses legitimate costs. In Texas, homeowners have 12 months from the storm date to file or supplement a claim.

**Phase 4 — Demolition of Damaged Materials**
Before rebuilding can begin, everything that's compromised must come out. This includes water-damaged drywall, saturated insulation, buckled flooring, charred framing, damaged siding, and deteriorated decking. Selective demolition is done carefully to avoid disturbing adjacent undamaged materials.

**Phase 5 — Structural Repair**
Any compromised structural elements — roof rafters, wall framing, floor joists, fascia boards, sill plates — are repaired or replaced before finish work begins. This is the foundation of a proper restoration.

**Phase 6 — Rebuild and Systems Coordination**
Once structure is confirmed sound, systems are coordinated: rough-in electrical and plumbing work is completed by licensed subcontractors before walls close. Insulation is replaced, vapor barriers are installed, and the building envelope is restored.

**Phase 7 — Finishing**
Interior: drywall, tape and texture, paint, trim, flooring, cabinet and fixture reinstallation.
Exterior: siding, trim, soffit and fascia, windows, doors, paint, hardscape repairs.

### Interior Restoration Work

**Water Damage (the most common interior restoration)**
When water enters through a compromised roof, around a window, or from a burst pipe, it follows gravity and spreads laterally through building materials. Drywall absorbs moisture and loses structural integrity. Insulation compresses and loses R-value. Wood framing swells and can develop mold within 24–72 hours under Texas heat and humidity. Interior water restoration involves extracting standing water (in severe cases), drying the structure with commercial dehumidifiers and air movers, removing damaged materials, treating any affected areas for microbial growth, and rebuilding.

**Ceiling Repair and Replacement**
Ceiling damage is often the first visible sign of interior water intrusion. Staining, sagging, and collapse are common after a roof leak or burst overhead pipe. Damaged drywall or plaster is removed to the nearest structural point, framing is inspected and repaired if needed, and the ceiling is rebuilt, textured, and painted to match.

**Drywall Repair and Replacement**
Fire, smoke, water, and impact events all damage drywall. Restoration requires removing sections to clean, unaffected edges, treating underlying structure if needed, hanging and finishing new drywall, and matching existing texture — which ranges from orange peel to knockdown to smooth finish in DFW homes.

**Interior Painting (Post-Damage)**
Post-restoration painting isn't cosmetic — it's the final seal on repaired surfaces. It also requires matching existing colors and sheens, which may require color matching of original paint in older DFW homes.

**Flooring Repair and Replacement**
Water damage destroys most flooring types. Hardwood buckles and cups. Laminate swells and separates. Tile grout absorbs moisture, and the substrate below may delaminate. Carpet padding traps water and becomes a mold substrate quickly in Texas summer temperatures. All damaged flooring is removed down to the subfloor, the subfloor is inspected and repaired, and new flooring is installed to match or replace existing.

**Cabinet and Countertop Restoration**
Kitchen and bathroom cabinets exposed to water or fire damage often require full replacement. Cabinet boxes absorb moisture and swell; face frames and doors warp. Countertops cracked or charred by heat are replaced. Insurance typically covers like-for-like replacement.

**Bathroom and Kitchen Restoration**
These rooms are most vulnerable to water damage given the concentration of supply and drain lines. Full restoration can involve tile demolition and replacement, vanity and fixture reinstallation, subfloor repair, and reestablishing waterproof membranes in shower surrounds.

**Structural Framing Repair**
Interior framing compromised by water infiltration, fire, or impact is sistered (reinforced alongside existing members), scabbed, or fully replaced depending on extent of damage. This is done before any insulation or drywall goes back.

**Insulation Replacement**
Batt insulation in walls and attics absorbs moisture and must be replaced after water events. Blown-in insulation in attics can also compact and develop mold. New insulation is installed to current energy code — typically R-38 in DFW attic spaces per Texas energy standards.

**Electrical and Plumbing Coordination**
Restoration contractors typically coordinate licensed electrical and plumbing subcontractors for systems work that opens up during the restoration. Water-damaged wiring must be inspected and often replaced. Burst supply lines are repaired before walls close.

**Mold Remediation Coordination**
When mold is present or suspected (often the case in DFW when water damage sat for more than 48 hours before being addressed), a licensed mold remediation contractor may be brought in before rebuild work begins. The restoration contractor coordinates sequencing so remediation is completed and cleared before new materials go in.

### Exterior Restoration Work

**Full Exterior Restoration**
After a significant storm event, the entire exterior of a DFW home may need attention simultaneously: roof, gutters, siding, trim, windows, doors, and exterior paint. A full exterior restoration manages all of these trades under one contract.

**Siding Replacement**
Hail impact, wind-driven debris, and extended moisture exposure all damage exterior siding. Vinyl siding cracks and punctures. Fiber cement siding chips and fractures at impact points. Wood siding rots when the paint film is compromised and moisture penetrates. Full house siding replacement involves removing all existing cladding, inspecting and repairing the sheathing beneath, installing new housewrap, and reinstalling new siding with proper flashing at all transitions.

**Window and Door Replacement**
Hail cracks window glazing and damages frames. Wind events break glass and blow in window units. Even when glass survives, hail impact on aluminum or vinyl frames damages seals and compromises thermal performance. Window and door replacement in a restoration context involves proper flashing and air-sealing to restore the building envelope.

**Porch and Patio Restoration**
Covered porches take direct hail hits and wind damage. Porch ceilings (often beadboard or tongue-and-groove) split and dent. Structural columns may crack or shift after high winds or falling trees. Post-storm porch restoration addresses structural integrity, ceiling replacement, and finish work.

**Deck Rebuild**
Storm damage — particularly from falling trees or branches — can destroy deck sections. Decks can also sustain damage from flood events that shift footings. Restoration involves removing damaged sections, repairing or replacing footings and framing, and matching the existing deck material and railing profile where possible.

**Fence Replacement**
Wood privacy fences in DFW are particularly vulnerable to straight-line wind events and tornadoes. Panel sections fail at post connections. Full sections blow over. Fence replacement after a storm is commonly covered under homeowner policies as other structures coverage. Posts, rails, and pickets are replaced to match existing configuration or upgraded.

**Garage Door Replacement**
Hail dents garage door panels. Wind events blow garage doors inward or off tracks. Beyond cosmetics, a damaged garage door compromises the building envelope and security. Garage door replacement in a restoration scope matches existing door dimensions, R-value, and configuration.

**Concrete and Driveway Repair**
Hail damage to concrete is less common but does occur with large hailstones. More frequently, driveway and sidewalk damage comes from tree removal or heavy equipment movement during storm cleanup. Crack repair, section replacement, and surface sealing are standard restoration tasks.

**Landscape Restoration**
Major storm events strip landscaping. Trees fall and damage sod, planting beds, and irrigation systems. While full landscaping is often excluded from homeowner policies, tree removal, stump grinding, and basic site restoration are sometimes included in the claim scope depending on the policy.

**Chimney Restoration**
Hail and wind events damage chimney caps, crown, flashing, and brick mortar. Chimney restoration in a roofing and restoration context typically addresses the crown (concrete cap at top), cap (metal rain cap), flashing at the roof transition, and repointing of damaged mortar joints. Significant structural brick damage requires a masonry contractor.

**Soffit and Fascia Replacement**
Wind gets under soffit panels and tears them away. Hail dents aluminum soffit and fascia. These elements are critical to keeping moisture and pests out of the attic space. Restoration involves replacing damaged sections and ensuring all ventilation baffles are reinstalled correctly.

**Shed and Outbuilding Restoration**
Outbuildings — sheds, workshops, pool houses — are covered as other structures under most homeowner policies (typically 10% of dwelling coverage). Storm damage to these structures is handled with the same assessment and rebuild process as the main house.

**Structural Exterior Repair**
Beyond cladding, some storm events damage the structural exterior of a home directly: broken rafters from fallen trees, cracked rim joists from flood events, compromised wall sheathing from sustained moisture. Structural exterior repair is done before any cladding or siding is reinstalled.

### Storm Restoration (Primary Specialization)

The DFW Metroplex sits in one of the most active severe weather corridors in the United States. Tarrant County — where Crowley is located — regularly experiences multiple hail-producing storms per year. Recent data shows:

- Crowley has experienced up to 302 storm reports in a 12-month period
- Maximum hail recorded near Crowley: 4.50 inches
- Maximum wind recorded near Crowley: 89 mph
- Doppler radar has detected hail at or near Crowley on more than 91 occasions in recent years
- Severe weather days in the area commonly exceed 36 per year

The April 2026 storm event produced quarter-sized hail in southern Tarrant County including Crowley and Burleson, with 60-mph wind gusts. These are not rare events — they are a routine feature of living in North Texas.

Storm restoration encompasses:
- Hail damage: roof shingles, siding, gutters, windows, HVAC equipment, decking
- Wind damage: blown-off roofing, torn siding, downed fences, damaged soffits, structural pressure damage
- Tornado damage: partial or complete structural loss requiring emergency stabilization and full rebuild
- Flood/heavy rain events: water intrusion from failed gutters, clogged downspouts, ground saturation, and overland flooding

### Water Damage Restoration

Water damage from roof leaks is the most direct consequence of storm damage that doesn't get addressed quickly. But water damage in DFW homes comes from multiple sources:

**Roof leak intrusion** — A compromised roof allows water to enter the attic and migrate into the home's interior. Insulation, sheathing, ceiling joists, and drywall are all at risk. Water that enters after a storm and isn't addressed promptly becomes a mold problem within days.

**Burst pipes from winter freeze events** — North Texas temperatures drop below freezing multiple times per winter season, sometimes severely. Winter Storm Uri (February 2021) drove temperatures across the Metroplex to single digits and held them there for days. Pipes inside exterior walls, in unconditioned attic spaces, and in garage utility rooms froze and burst across hundreds of thousands of DFW homes. When a pipe bursts, the damage isn't always immediately obvious — water travels inside walls and floors before it becomes visible. Many homeowners returned after the storm to find soaked drywall, destroyed flooring, and standing water in lower levels. Crowley and southwest Fort Worth were among the hardest hit areas. Freeze-related pipe failures will continue to be a restoration need every time DFW experiences a significant winter weather event.

**Plumbing failures** — Supply line failures under sinks and behind toilets, failed water heaters, and deteriorating drain lines in older DFW homes (particularly those built in the 1970s–1990s with galvanized or polybutylene supply piping) cause significant interior water damage.

### Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration

Residential fires in DFW follow a consistent post-incident pattern. The fire itself destroys structural elements and finishes in affected rooms. Smoke and soot travel throughout the structure via HVAC systems and air gaps, depositing acidic residues on all surfaces — including rooms that never saw flame. Water from firefighting efforts saturates the structure, often causing secondary damage across unaffected areas.

Fire restoration involves:
- Emergency stabilization and board-up
- Smoke and soot removal from all surfaces (including HVAC ductwork)
- Odor elimination using industrial air scrubbers and thermal fogging
- Demolition of charred and smoke-saturated materials
- Structural assessment and repair of fire-damaged framing
- Coordination with mold remediation if firefighting water caused prolonged moisture exposure
- Full rebuild of affected interior and exterior areas

### Texas Insurance Restoration Process

Working a Texas insurance claim requires specific knowledge of how the process functions at the state level.

**How Texas claims typically work:**
1. Homeowner files a claim with their insurance carrier
2. Insurance company assigns an adjuster who inspects the property
3. Adjuster produces a Xactimate estimate defining the scope and dollar amount the carrier will pay
4. Contractor reviews the adjuster's estimate for missed line items, undervalued pricing, or code-required upgrades not included
5. Contractor submits a supplement — a formal request for additional coverage with documentation

**The supplement process** is a legal and standard practice in the restoration industry. Most initial adjuster estimates miss legitimate line items: proper code requirements for current building codes, miscellaneous debris removal, overhead and profit on complex jobs, code-required upgrades when replacing systems, and line items for work not visible during the initial inspection. A contractor who knows Xactimate and the supplement process can recover hundreds to thousands of dollars in legitimate costs that the initial estimate did not include.

**Texas-specific claim considerations:**
- Texas homeowners generally have 12 months from the date of loss to file or supplement a claim; waiting longer than 60 days from damage discovery gives carriers stronger grounds to argue wear and tear vs. storm damage
- Texas law gives homeowners rights in the claims process; carriers must acknowledge claims promptly and pay undisputed amounts
- Many DFW storms generate widespread claims across thousands of properties, which can slow adjuster response times — having a contractor already engaged helps move the process forward
- Texas contractors working insurance restoration jobs are prohibited from waiving deductibles (this is a violation of state law and can constitute insurance fraud)

### Mold Remediation Context

Texas heat and humidity create ideal conditions for rapid mold growth after any water intrusion event. DFW summer temperatures in the 90s to low 100s, combined with interior humidity from a compromised building envelope, can produce visible mold growth within 24–72 hours of water exposure.

Key points:
- Mold is a near-certain outcome when water damage sits untreated in a DFW home during summer months
- Mold remediation in Texas must be performed by a contractor licensed by the Texas Department of Insurance (TDI) and the Texas Department of State Health Services (DSHS)
- A restoration contractor coordinates remediation sequencing: mold out first, clearance testing completed, then rebuild begins — never the reverse
- Insurance claims for mold are sometimes contested; having documentation of the triggering event (storm, burst pipe) and timeline is essential for claim success

### Aging Home Restoration

The DFW suburbs saw rapid growth from the 1970s through the 2000s. Crowley and surrounding areas in southwest Tarrant County contain significant housing stock from the 1980s and 1990s. These homes present recurring restoration needs:

- Original wood siding or vinyl siding installed before modern fiber cement products are at end of life
- Single-pane or early double-pane windows with failed seals and degraded frames
- Original roof systems that have been repaired multiple times and are at or past their designed lifespan
- Galvanized steel or polybutylene plumbing supply lines susceptible to failure
- Aluminum wiring in some 1970s-era homes that requires coordination with a licensed electrician during restoration
- Original insulation in attics that doesn't meet current energy code minimums

When a storm event triggers a restoration claim on an aging home, the scope often expands beyond the immediate damage to address pre-existing deficiencies that the new work must tie into or match. A knowledgeable restoration contractor documents what is storm-caused vs. pre-existing carefully, which is critical for both claim accuracy and contractor liability.

### Full-Property Restoration After Catastrophic Events

Tornado events are a distinct category. When a tornado strikes a DFW neighborhood, the result can be partial or complete structural loss. Post-tornado restoration involves:

- Emergency stabilization of remaining structure (shoring, tarping, board-up)
- Coordination with structural engineers for assessment
- Complete exterior rebuild: roof, walls, windows, siding
- Interior rebuild from framing out
- Coordination with all licensed trades: electrical, plumbing, HVAC
- Permitting with local municipality (City of Crowley, Tarrant County)
- Extended insurance claim management over a multi-month rebuild timeline
