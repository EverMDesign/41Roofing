## City Overview

### Quick Facts
- **County**: Johnson County (primary); northern portions extend into Tarrant County
- **Population**: ~55,220 (2024 estimate); 47,641 per 2020 Census
- **Growth rate**: 151.3% growth since 2000 — one of the fastest-growing suburbs in DFW
- **Largest city** in Johnson County
- **Location**: Approximately 14 miles south of downtown Fort Worth along I-35W
- **Part of**: Dallas-Fort Worth-Arlington MSA
- **Incorporated**: 1912 (incorporated as a town); city status followed with sustained growth
- **Character**: Family-oriented suburban community with a strong small-town identity, active HOA culture, and rapid new-construction growth along the southern and eastern edges of the city

### General Character
- Strong sense of community pride; residents identify deeply with Burleson ISD schools and local sports
- Mix of established 1990s-2000s subdivisions and brand-new master-planned communities
- Affordable alternative to Fort Worth and Mansfield with quick Chisholm Trail Parkway access
- Active faith community — numerous churches anchor neighborhood identity
- Growing retail and commercial corridor along I-35W (Wilshire Boulevard) and McAlister Road

## History

### Founding (1881-1900)
- **1881**: Missouri-Kansas-Texas (MKT) Railroad planned a line from Fort Worth to Hillsboro and established a depot on a site purchased from Rev. Henry C. Renfro
- Rev. Renfro named the depot **Burleson** in honor of **Dr. Rufus C. Burleson**, president of Baylor University and Renfro's mentor
- **1882**: First post office established (located in a saloon); John L. Dickey served as first postmaster
- By 1890: Population of 200; community supported grocers, druggists, general stores, and cotton gin-gristmills
- **Cotton** was the dominant economic driver in early decades

### Early 20th Century
- Population hovered at 368 in 1904, then declined to 241 in the 1920s
- Slow, steady growth through mid-century: 573 residents by 1940
- Economy centered on agriculture, livestock, and small-town retail services
- The Great Depression limited growth, but community institutions (churches, schools) stabilized the town

### Post-WWII Suburban Expansion
- After World War II, returning veterans and growing families pushed Fort Worth's suburbs outward
- Burleson positioned itself as an affordable, family-friendly alternative to Fort Worth proper
- **I-35W** became the backbone of suburban growth connecting Burleson directly to Fort Worth
- By **1980**: 11,734 residents and 196 business establishments
- By **2000**: 20,976 residents
- The **Chisholm Trail Parkway** (opened 2014) dramatically accelerated southern growth — new subdivisions and master-planned communities launched along its length

### Modern Growth Era (2000-Present)
- Population doubled from 2000 to 2013 (~40,000)
- Crossed 47,000 by the 2020 Census; estimated at 55,000+ by 2024
- Growth driven by: affordable land, strong Burleson ISD reputation, Chisholm Trail Parkway access, and DFW job market expansion
- Ongoing master-planned development (Tallgrass, etc.) continues to add thousands of new homes annually
- City annexed surrounding areas, extending into both Johnson and Tarrant counties

## Weather & Storm Patterns

### Climate Overview
- **Climate classification**: Humid subtropical (Cfa)
- **Average annual rainfall**: ~37 inches (940 mm)
- **Temperature range**: 36°F to 97°F typical; rarely below 24°F or above 102°F
- **Hottest months**: July and August — highs averaging 95°F, lows around 72°F
- **Coldest months**: December and January — highs averaging 56°F, lows around 34°F
- Summers are hot and humid; winters are cold and occasionally severe

### Hail Frequency — Critical for Roofing
- Burleson sits squarely within the **I-35 hail corridor**, one of the most hail-active zones in North Texas
- Doppler radar has detected hail at or near Burleson **130+ times** based on historical tracking data
- **38 severe weather warnings** in recent 12-month periods; 35+ ground-level hail reports by trained spotters
- **2025 alone**: 43 documented hail events, some producing golf ball- to softball-sized stones
- **June 2023**: Baseball-size hail caused widespread roof, gutter, and A/C damage across Joshua and south Burleson — every home in affected neighborhoods reported damage
- **April 2026**: A major severe weather outbreak hit the southwest DFW corridor, placing Burleson in one of the heaviest impact zones
- Even 1-inch hail can crack asphalt shingles and dent metal roofing

### Severe Weather Season
- **Peak storm season**: March through June
- Severe weather pattern: Warm, moist Gulf air collides with cool, dry air masses from the northwest — ideal conditions for supercell thunderstorms producing large hail, damaging straight-line winds (60-80+ mph), and tornadoes
- Secondary storm threats occur in October-November

### Tornado Risk
- Johnson County (Burleson's home county) has confirmed tornado activity
- May-June 2026 outbreak confirmed **6 tornadoes** in the county, including EF0 and EF1 ratings
- Burleson is not in the most extreme tornado belt, but risk is real and recurring every spring

### Winter Weather Risks
- **February 2021 (Winter Storm Uri)**: All 254 Texas counties impacted; the costliest winter storm in Texas history ($195 billion in damages); 246+ deaths statewide
  - North Texas experienced record low temperatures, heavy snow, and widespread ice
  - Ice accumulation caused attic condensation issues, ice dam formation, and accelerated wear on aging shingles; burst pipes led to interior water damage requiring roof and ceiling repair
  - Ice-covered roofs added extreme structural load — older homes in Burleson with aging decking were particularly vulnerable
- Winter ice storms (less severe than Uri) occur periodically — freezing rain, sleet, and brief sub-20°F periods are not uncommon
- Thermal cycling (freeze-thaw) causes shingle granule loss and flashing separation over time

### How North Texas Climate Affects Roofing Materials
- **UV damage**: 220+ sunny days per year; UV radiation degrades asphalt binders, causing shingles to become brittle and crack
- **Heat expansion**: Summer temperatures above 90°F cause roofing materials to expand and contract daily, working fasteners loose and stressing seams over time
- **Wind uplift**: Severe thunderstorm winds routinely exceed 60-70 mph; standard 3-tab shingles particularly vulnerable; architectural and Class 4 impact-resistant shingles recommended
- **Hail impact**: The single largest cause of roof insurance claims in the region; damages shingles, dents gutters, cracks ridge caps, and compromises vent covers
- **Humidity**: High summer humidity promotes algae and moss growth on north-facing roof surfaces — shortens effective shingle lifespan without treatment

## Roofing & Restoration Industry Relevance

### Common Roofing Materials in Burleson
- **Architectural (laminated) asphalt shingles** — Dominant material across 80%+ of Burleson residential stock; 30-40 year rated lifespan; aesthetically versatile
- **3-tab asphalt shingles** — Found on older homes (pre-2000); at or past end of life for homes built in the 1980s-1990s; being phased out in favor of architectural shingles on replacements
- **Class 4 impact-resistant shingles** — Growing in adoption due to hail frequency; often qualifies homeowners for insurance premium discounts (Texas law allows insurers to incentivize Class 4 material)
- **Standing seam metal roofing** — Growing popularity in newer custom homes and commercial properties; wind uplift ratings of 130+ mph; higher upfront cost but superior longevity
- **Corrugated metal** — Common on agricultural structures, older rural-area homes in the Burleson fringe
- **Concrete and clay tile** — Found in higher-end subdivisions; less common than shingles but present in premium construction

### Typical Home Ages and Replacement Cycles
- **Pre-1990 homes**: Located primarily near Old Town and early subdivisions; original roofs are past their useful life; prime replacement candidates
- **1990-2005 homes**: The largest cohort of Burleson's housing stock; homes in this range are 20-35+ years old — at or entering the standard replacement window for asphalt shingles
- **2005-2015 homes**: Mid-cycle; more likely to need hail damage repairs or insurance-driven replacements than full elective replacements
- **2015-present new construction**: New roofs but still subject to hail and wind damage; insurance claims rather than age-driven replacement
- **Key driver**: Burleson's rapid 2000s growth means a large number of homes are simultaneously entering replacement age — high volume market opportunity

### HOA Considerations
- Burleson has **50+ registered homeowner associations** — one of the highest concentrations in the DFW suburban market
- HOA covenants commonly govern:
  - Approved roofing materials (typically architectural shingles or better; no 3-tab)
  - Approved colors (must match or complement neighborhood palette)
  - Installation timeline requirements after damage
  - Contractor licensing and insurance requirements
- **Tallgrass** and other master-planned communities have particularly detailed material approval processes
- Always obtain HOA approval in writing before beginning work — failure to comply can result in fines or required removal

### Building Codes and Permit Requirements
- City of Burleson adopted the **2021 International Building Codes** (November 2023)
- **Permits are required for roof replacements** in Burleson
- Permits and Inspections contact: **817-426-9632**
- Code requirements cover: ventilation standards, decking specifications, and installation methods
- Northern sections of Burleson (in Tarrant County) may have different jurisdictional requirements — verify county at project outset
- Texas does not have a statewide roofing license, but Burleson contractors must carry appropriate insurance and pull permits

### Insurance Claim Patterns
- Hail damage is a **covered peril** under standard Texas homeowners insurance policies
- Most Texas homeowners policies allow **one year from the date of loss** to file a claim
- Two payout structures in the market:
  - **Replacement Cost Value (RCV)**: Covers full replacement cost; preferred for homeowners
  - **Actual Cash Value (ACV)**: Subtracts depreciation; results in lower payouts; common on older roofs
- After major hail events, demand for free inspections surges immediately — storms in spring 2023, 2025, and 2026 all triggered high claim volumes
- County officials have cautioned residents to document damage with a licensed roofer before contacting insurers to prevent underpayment
- **Supplemental claims** are common when initial adjustments miss items (gutters, ridge caps, vent covers, fascia)
