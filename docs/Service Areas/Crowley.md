# Crowley, Texas - Service Area Profile

---

## City Overview

- **County**: Tarrant County, Texas
- **Location**: Approximately 15 miles south of downtown Fort Worth; two miles west of Interstate Highway 35W at the crossroads of FM 1187 and FM 731
- **2026 Estimated Population**: ~21,550 (up from 18,070 at the 2020 census — a 40.8% increase from 2010)
- **Incorporation**: Town incorporated February 3, 1951; redesignated as a city September 3, 1972
- **General Character**: Family-oriented suburban city in southern Tarrant County. Crowley blends established older neighborhoods near downtown with rapidly growing master-planned subdivisions along the Chisholm Trail Parkway corridor. It is known for a strong school district, affordable housing relative to north DFW, and a tight-knit community identity.

### Neighborhoods and Subdivisions

Crowley has 22+ registered homeowners associations, reflecting a residential fabric that spans multiple decades of suburban development. Key subdivisions and areas relevant for residential roofing work:

- **Deer Creek Estates** — Established, family-friendly neighborhood with mature trees and well-maintained homes; heavily-trafficked by roofing companies after hail events
- **Creekside** — Mid-sized homes in a mature neighborhood setting; strong community pool and park presence
- **Chisholm Trail Ranch** — Master-planned development near Chisholm Trail Parkway and McPherson Boulevard; approximately 1,100 single-family homes in Crowley ISD; newer construction (2010s–2020s)
- **Karis** — Growing new-construction community with modern homes and amenity packages
- **Mayfair Estates** — Mid-sized to large homes, primarily late 1980s construction; prime age range for roof replacement
- **Summer Trails** — Residential subdivision with mix of home ages
- **Crowley West** — Rural-character neighborhood on the western edge of the city boundary
- **Historic Downtown Area** — Older ranch-style homes and modest residences dating to the 1950s–1980s, many on original city lots

## Local Landmarks & Points of Interest

### Parks and Recreation
- **Bicentennial Park** (900 E. Glendale) — The city's central community hub. Hosts the annual Celebration of Freedom (Fourth of July), Christmas in Crowley, and other major city events. Features walking trails, picnic areas, sports fields, and a splash pad.
- **Dionne Bagsby All Sports Complex** — Multi-sport facility used heavily by youth athletics organizations
- **Rosemary Ridge Park** — Neighborhood park serving south Crowley residential areas
- **Crowley Veteran's Memorial Plaza** — Community landmark honoring local veterans; strong community pride symbol

### Schools (Crowley ISD)
Crowley ISD serves 17,000+ students across 27 campuses:
- **Crowley High School** — Eagles (purple and white); home of Eagle Stadium
- **North Crowley High School** — Panthers (royal blue and silver)
- Four middle schools, 16 elementary schools, a Montessori Academy, and a Career & Technology Education Center
- Schools are a primary community anchor; neighborhoods within Crowley ISD boundaries carry strong homeowner demand

### Churches and Community Anchors
- **St. Theresa Catholic Church and School** — Notable historic parish with a school; a longstanding institution in the community
- Multiple Baptist, non-denominational, and evangelical congregations serving the south Tarrant County community

### Shopping and Commercial Areas
- **Crowley Crossing Plaza** — Downtown gathering and retail node; host of Christmas in Crowley and the Fall Festival
- **Chisholm Trail Parkway Corridor** — Rapidly developing commercial strip with retail, restaurants, and services catering to new subdivisions
- Proximity to **Hulen Mall** (~20 minutes north in Fort Worth) and **Burleson retail centers** to the south

### Historic Landmarks
- **Historic Main Street / Downtown Crowley** — Retains original small-town character with some buildings dating to the early 1900s
- **Original Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe Railway Depot Site** — The 1885 depot site is part of Crowley's founding identity; the railroad corridor shaped the original town grid
- **NAS JRB Fort Worth (Naval Air Station Joint Reserve Base)** — Major military installation immediately north of Crowley; a significant community and economic presence

## History

- **circa 1848**: Pioneer settlers begin farming the area around Deer Creek in what is now southern Tarrant County
- **1882**: A post office is established, formalizing the settlement
- **1885**: The Gulf, Colorado and Santa Fe Railway lays tracks and builds the first station depot. The community is named for **S.H. Crowley**, the railroad's master of transportation. The railroad enabled efficient shipping of crops and livestock and anchored the town's economic identity
- **Early 1900s**: Dairy farming, ranching, and railroad-related commerce become the economic backbone
- **Late 1920s–1930s**: Population stabilizes at approximately 275 residents
- **Post-WWII**: Population reaches 500; modest suburban growth begins as Fort Worth expands southward
- **1951**: Town formally incorporates, enabling access to municipal infrastructure
- **1960s**: Municipal utilities (water, natural gas, sewage) are installed following incorporation, enabling residential growth
- **1970**: Population reaches 2,583; suburban expansion accelerates
- **1972**: Redesignated from a town to a city
- **1980**: Population doubles to 5,852, driven by DFW suburban sprawl moving south from Fort Worth
- **1980s–2000s**: Major residential subdivision development; neighborhoods like Mayfair Estates, Deer Creek Estates, and Creekside are built
- **2000–2009**: Approximately 33% of current housing stock added during this decade; city transitions from small town to mid-size suburb
- **2014**: **Chisholm Trail Parkway** opens, dramatically improving connectivity between Crowley, southwest Fort Worth, and downtown. This triggers a new wave of residential and commercial development along the corridor
- **2020s**: Population surpasses 18,000 (2020 census) and continues climbing. Master-planned communities like Chisholm Trail Ranch bring 1,000+ new homes. The Chisholm Trail Parkway is slated for a 13-mile widening project ($250M), with completion expected by late 2028, projecting continued growth through 2050

## Weather & Storm Patterns

### Climate Baseline
- **Annual Rainfall**: Approximately 36 inches per year
- **Temperature Range**: Typically 36°F–96°F; rarely below 24°F or above 102°F
- **Summer**: Hot and humid; August averages a high of 95.7°F
- **Winter**: Cold and windy; January averages a low of 38.7°F; approximately 85 days per year below 50°F
- **General Pattern**: Partly cloudy year-round with distinct severe weather seasons

### Hail and Tornado Risk
- Crowley sits squarely in **"Hail Alley"** — one of the most hail-active corridors in the United States, stretching from North Texas through Central Texas
- The DFW Metroplex regularly experiences **golf ball-sized hail (1.75 inches)** and larger during severe spring and summer storms
- In a recent 12-month period, there were **302 storm reports** near Crowley with maximum hail of **4.50 inches** and maximum winds of **89 mph** across 36 severe weather days
- Tornado risk is real; EF-0 tornadoes have touched down near Crowley (including near NAS JRB) causing roof damage, outbuilding damage, and fence damage

### Severe Weather Season
- **Primary season**: March through June (spring storm season)
- **Secondary season**: September through October (fall convective storms)
- Hailstorms can and do occur outside of spring, but the most damaging events are concentrated in March–May

### Notable Historic Storm Events
- **May 5, 1995** — Catastrophic hailstorm across Fort Worth and Tarrant County; hailstones up to 4 inches wide; damages exceeded **$1 billion**; one of the most costly hail events in Texas history
- **April 10, 2001** — Southern Tarrant County hit by intense hail and high winds; major wave of roof damage insurance claims across Crowley's surrounding area
- **2022** — EF-0 tornado with 85 mph winds near the Briarcreeks area caused minor roof damage and outbuilding damage; straight-line winds caused shingle damage in multiple neighborhoods
- **June 2025** — Severe hailstorm struck DFW hard; Tarrant County among the worst-affected areas
- **April 25–26, 2026** — Severe storm system dropped half-dollar to baseball-sized hail (up to 3 inches) across Tarrant County, including reports near Arlington and the southern Tarrant corridor

### Climate Impact on Roofing Materials
- **Heat expansion and UV degradation**: Summers regularly exceed 100°F; asphalt shingles experience thermal cycling that cracks sealant strips and accelerates granule loss over time
- **Hail bruising**: Even moderate 1-inch hail can bruise shingle surfaces, compromising waterproofing without creating visible cracks — damage that is not visible from the ground but is documentable during inspection
- **Wind uplift**: 70–89 mph straight-line wind events can lift shingle tabs, break ridge caps, and cause partial blow-offs on older roofs
- **Ice storm risk**: Winter ice storms occur approximately every 2–4 years in Tarrant County (notable events in 2021 and 2023); ice damming and freeze-thaw cycles stress shingles and flashing
- **Overall**: A roof in Crowley faces heat, UV, hail, wind, and occasional ice — all four primary roofing stressors — making a 15–20 year lifespan common even for quality architectural shingles

## Roofing & Restoration Industry Relevance

### Common Roofing Materials
- **Architectural (dimensional) asphalt shingles**: By far the most common material across Crowley's residential market; found on the vast majority of homes built from the 1980s through the present
- **Class 4 impact-resistant shingles**: Growing in adoption due to insurance premium discounts (15–30%) available from many Texas carriers; increasingly required by HOAs in newer subdivisions
- **Metal roofing (standing seam and corrugated)**: Gaining popularity among homeowners seeking long-term durability; more common on newer custom builds along the Chisholm Trail corridor
- **Concrete and clay tile**: Less common; found on higher-end custom homes; more resistant to hail but susceptible to cracking under direct large-hail impact
- **3-tab shingles**: Found on older homes (pre-2000); largely being replaced during storm-damage claims

### Home Ages and Replacement Cycles
- **Pre-1980 homes** (downtown/original town core): Roofs on these properties have often been replaced multiple times; some still have original decking; high likelihood of worn-out or improperly repaired systems
- **1980s–1990s homes** (Mayfair Estates, early Deer Creek, etc.): This is the primary roof replacement sweet spot — roofs in this age range (25–40+ years) are at or past the end of their designed lifespan; many have survived multiple severe storm seasons
- **2000s homes**: Depending on storm history and installation quality, these are entering the 15–25 year range where inspection and assessment is warranted; granule loss and sealant failure common
- **2010s–2020s new construction** (Chisholm Trail Ranch, Karis): Newer roofs but not immune to hail damage; storm events document clean, concentrated damage that makes insurance claims straightforward

### HOA Considerations
- 22+ registered HOAs in Crowley means that a significant portion of roofing work requires HOA approval for material and color selection
- Many newer HOA communities (especially along the Chisholm Trail corridor) require:
  - Specific shingle colors matching community standards
  - Minimum shingle grade (Class 4 IR increasingly required)
  - Prior written approval before work begins
  - Licensed and insured contractors only
- Established older neighborhoods (Deer Creek, Creekside) may have less restrictive HOAs or deed restrictions that have lapsed
- Always advise homeowners to check CC&Rs before signing a contract; 41 Roofing should be prepared to work within HOA approval processes

### Insurance Claim Patterns
- Hail damage is the **most common cause of residential roofing insurance claims** in Tarrant County
- Texas homeowners have a **2-year window** to file a property insurance claim after storm damage (per Texas Insurance Code)
- Common claim pattern: A storm event generates a surge of door-knocking by storm chasers; 41 Roofing should position for rapid post-storm deployment with documented inspection reports
- Many homeowners are unaware that interior granule loss and shingle bruising constitutes a valid claim; educational marketing ("Free Storm Inspection") is highly effective in this market
- Insurance carriers increasingly offer reduced premiums for Class 4 shingles, which creates an upgrade selling opportunity on every claim

### Building Permits and Codes
- **Permit Authority**: City of Crowley Community Development Department — Permit and Building Inspections
  - Phone: 817-297-2201 ext. 3000 (permits) / ext. 3020 (inspections)
  - Hours: Monday–Thursday, 7:30 AM–5:30 PM
- Roofing replacements require a permit in Crowley; always pull permits on full replacements
- The city adopted updated zoning and subdivision regulations (Chapter 98 and Chapter 106) in 2022
- Texas follows the **International Residential Code (IRC)** with state amendments; Crowley enforces these locally
- Minimum requirements: proper underlayment, drip edge, ice and water shield in applicable zones, correct fastening patterns
- 41 Roofing should be registered/licensed with the city and carry appropriate Tarrant County contractor credentials
